\subsection{Further related work}\label{app:related-extended}
Pixel-space generative world models learn action-controllable video simulators, from navigation (NWM;
\citealp{bar2025nwm}) to foundation models for physical AI (Cosmos; \citealp{nvidia2025cosmos}) and interactive
environments (Genie; \citealp{bruce2024genie}), and model-based reinforcement learning learns task-specific latent
dynamics jointly with rewards and policies \citep{hafner2019planet,hafner2025dreamerv3,hansen2024tdmpc2}.
%
These systems optimise sample fidelity or online return with their own encoders and data, so they do not share our
setting of a frozen encoder with targets in common feature coordinates, and we treat them as complementary.
%
Surgical world modelling is likewise driven by generative video models, such as Endora for endoscopy
video~\citep{li2024endora}, SurgWM with latent-action control~\citep{koju2025surgwm} and Cosmos-H-Surgical for policy
learning~\citep{he2025cosmoshsurgical}, whereas we use Open-H dVRK recordings~\citep{openh2026embodiment} to test
kinematics-conditioned forecasting in feature space.

\section{Extended method}\label{app:method}
\subsection{Model details and hyperparameters}\label{app:impl}
The memory encoder attends over all $H\cdot N{=}768$ tokens of the three observed grids, each query-decoder layer
applies self-attention, cross-attention to memory and an MLP under AdaLN-zero, and actions are embedded by a 2-layer MLP
and a single-layer GRU of width $d$.
%
The transport uses key dimension 64 and a $7{\times}7$ window over $S{=}3$ source frames (147 candidates per query), and
the correction head applies LayerNorm, a zero-initialised linear map to $C$ and a per-channel scale initialised at 1.

\subsection{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}
Throughout, $c=(\Z_{\le 0},\mathbf{a}_{<k})$ is the context, $\mathbf{e}_j$ the $j$-th standard basis vector, and
$\mathbf{z}_{0,j}$ the $j$-th row of $\Z_0$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:bayes}]
\emph{Optimal forecast.} Under squared error the optimal forecast is the conditional mean. Since $\Z_0$ is determined
by $c$ and the new content has zero conditional mean, the tower property gives
\begin{align}
\E[\Z_k\mid c]
  &= \E[\mathbf{P}_k\mid c]\,\Z_0+\E\big[\E[\bm{\varepsilon}_k\mid\mathbf{P}_k,c]\,\big|\,c\big]
   = \bar{\mathbf{P}}_k\Z_0 .
\end{align}
\emph{Structure of $\bar{\mathbf{P}}_k$.} Row $i$ of $\mathbf{P}_k$ is $\mathbf{e}_{\rho(i)}^{\top}$, so row $i$ of
$\bar{\mathbf{P}}_k$ is $\sum_{j}\Pr[\rho(i)=j\mid c]\,\mathbf{e}_j^{\top}$, a probability vector whose support lies in
$\mathcal{W}(i)$ whenever the support of $\rho(i)$ does.

\emph{Realisation by transport.} With $g=1$ and $\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{0}$, \cref{eq:output} gives
$\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{k,i}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{W}(i)}\pi_{k,i}(j)\,\mathbf{z}_{0,j}$. Let $\mathcal{S}_i$ be the support of
row $i$ of $\bar{\mathbf{P}}_k$, $u=|\mathcal{W}(i)\setminus\mathcal{S}_i|$, and choose logits
$\ell_j=\log\bar{P}_{k,ij}$ for $j\in\mathcal{S}_i$ and $\ell_j=-\Lambda$ otherwise. Since $\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}_i}\bar{P}_{k,ij}=1$,
\begin{align}
\pi_{k,i}(j)=\frac{\bar{P}_{k,ij}}{1+u e^{-\Lambda}}\;\;(j\in\mathcal{S}_i),\qquad
\pi_{k,i}(j)=\frac{e^{-\Lambda}}{1+u e^{-\Lambda}}\;\;(j\notin\mathcal{S}_i),
\end{align}
so the total-variation distance to row $i$ of $\bar{\mathbf{P}}_k$ is $2ue^{-\Lambda}/(1+ue^{-\Lambda})\le 2ue^{-\Lambda}$ and
\begin{align}
\big\|\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{k,i}-(\bar{\mathbf{P}}_k\Z_0)_i\big\|\;\le\;2u\,e^{-\Lambda}\max_{j\in\mathcal{W}(i)}\|\mathbf{z}_{0,j}\|
\;\xrightarrow[\Lambda\to\infty]{}\;0 .
\end{align}
With $S>1$ source frames the same argument holds with $\mathcal{W}(i)$ ranging over patches of all observed frames.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Regression target of \cref{sec:theory}]
An additive head outputs $\Z_0+\mathbf{R}$. Matching $\mathbf{P}\Z_0$ requires
\begin{align}
\mathbf{R}=(\mathbf{P}-\mathbf{I})\Z_0,\qquad
\mathbf{R}_{i}=\mathbf{z}_{0,\rho(i)}-\mathbf{z}_{0,i},\qquad
\|\mathbf{R}\|_F^2=\sum_i\big\|\mathbf{z}_{0,\rho(i)}-\mathbf{z}_{0,i}\big\|^2 .
\end{align}
For \ours{}, take the one-hot transport $\pi_{k,i}=\mathbf{e}_{\rho(i)}$ (the limit $\Lambda\to\infty$ above, admissible since
$\rho(i)\in\mathcal{W}(i)$) and $g=1$. Then $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{k,i}=\mathbf{z}_{0,\rho(i)}=(\mathbf{P}\Z_0)_i$ for every $i$,
so the required correction is $\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{0}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Compounding bound of \cref{sec:theory}]
Let the recursive rollout be $\hat{\Z}_j=f(\hat{\Z}_{j-1})$ with $\hat{\Z}_0=\Z_0$, the truth
$\Z_j=f(\Z_{j-1})+\bm{\eta}_j$ with $\|\bm{\eta}_j\|\le\delta$, and $e_j=\|\hat{\Z}_j-\Z_j\|$. By the triangle inequality
and the Lipschitz property,
\begin{align}
e_j\;\le\;\big\|f(\hat{\Z}_{j-1})-f(\Z_{j-1})\big\|+\|\bm{\eta}_j\|\;\le\;L\,e_{j-1}+\delta,\qquad e_0=0 .
\end{align}
By induction on $k$ (the case $k=1$ is $e_1\le\delta$; if $e_{k-1}\le\delta\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}L^j$ then
$e_k\le L\delta\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}L^j+\delta$),
\begin{align}
e_k\;\le\;\delta\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}L^j\;=\;
\begin{cases}\delta\,\dfrac{L^k-1}{L-1}, & L\neq 1,\\[4pt] k\,\delta, & L=1,\end{cases}
\end{align}
which grows geometrically for $L>1$. A direct predictor forms $\hat{\Z}_k$ from the observed inputs
$(\Z_{\le 0},\mathbf{a})$ alone, so its error at horizon $k$ is its per-horizon error $\delta_k$ and does not depend on
its predictions at other horizons.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Plug-in heads for open-source world models}\label{app:plugin}
\input{submission_folder/tables/plugin.tex}
For an open-source backbone with prediction $\mathbf{B}_k$, the head outputs $\hat\Z_k=(1-g_k)\mathbf{B}_k+g_k\mathbf{T}_k$,
where $\mathbf{T}_k$ is the local $7{\times}7$ soft transport, with identity bias, of \emph{observed} encoder tokens.
%
The gate $g_k=\sigma(\mathbf{w}^\top\h+b)$ starts at $\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{0}$ and $b=-4$, so training starts numerically
from the unmodified backbone, and nothing else in the backbone, loss or schedule changes.
%
\Cref{tab:plugin} gives the resulting errors.

\textbf{V-JEPA 2-AC.} Queries come from the predictor's normalised hidden state for each output block (1024-D), and
keys and values from the layer-normalised ViT-g/16 tokens ($16{\times}16$, 1408-D) of the last $S{=}3$ observed frames,
with a learned per-source key offset (0.16M parameters).
%
The fine-tuning objective is the released one (teacher-forced L1 plus rollout L1), and during rollouts the predictor
consumes $\hat\Z_k$ as its own prediction, while $\mathbf{T}_k$ only ever reads observed tokens.

\textbf{DINO-WM.} We subclass the official \texttt{ViTPredictor}, otherwise unmodified.
%
Queries come from the predictor's output token, and keys and values from its input token at the same slot on the
$14{\times}14$ DINOv2 patch grid ($S{=}1$); proprioceptive and action channels are left untouched.
%
After the first rollout step, that input token is DINO-WM's own previous prediction, so the head then moves predicted
rather than observed features, unlike \ours{} and the V-JEPA 2-AC head.
%
The extra parameters are drawn from a private random generator, so data order, initialisation and dropout of the base
predictor are identical in both arms.

\subsection{Datasets and splits}\label{app:data}
\input{submission_folder/tables/datasets.tex}
\Cref{tab:datasets} summarises the datasets.
%
\textbf{DROID.} We use 24 official full-release shards with 1{,}126 episodes from 14 sites, split by recording session
with a salted hash (316/59/58 sessions in train/val/test).
%
Frames are subsampled from 15 to 3\,Hz, each action block concatenates the 5 commanded poses and gripper positions of
one step, and the second exterior camera records the same instants from another viewpoint.
%
Two of the 132 DROID test episodes are shorter than one $H{+}K{=}13$-step window, so per-episode analyses use 130.
%
\textbf{Open-H Hamlyn.} The \texttt{observation.images.color} stream, which the release labels ``endoscope'', is an Intel
RealSense scene camera ($848{\times}480$).
%
Each action block holds five commands, each an 8-D pose-and-gripper target per arm; 6 of 1{,}019 episodes are shorter
than 14 steps and removed, and splits are 70/15/15 per task by a hash of the episode id.
%
\textbf{Language-Table.} We use 14 of the 1{,}024 Open-X shards of a fixed release (6{,}161 episodes) at the native
10\,Hz and keep the 2{,}589 episodes long enough for $H{+}K{=}13$ steps, split 81/9/10\% by a salted hash of the episode id.
%
The release has no id of the source play episode, so segments of one play session can fall into different splits; for
only 1 of 264 test segments does a training segment start or end with a frame closer to the test boundary frame than
the median one-step frame change.
%
\textbf{IWS.} We use the IWS subset of the RLA-WM release
\citep{wang2026iws,zhang2026rlawm}, real bimanual recordings of pushing a T-block, handling a box and manipulating a rope
from one RGB camera at 30\,fps ($640{\times}480$, resized to $224{\times}224$).
%
One model step groups five native command rows (1/6\,s), so the official 60-row horizon is $K{=}12$ steps (2\,s), and
the action block concatenates the five recorded command vectors (4-D for PushT, 14-D for box, 8-D for rope).
%
The test set is the 200 official validation handles per task, drawn from 10 reserved recordings, and each handle
provides a single observed frame, which we replicate into the $H$-frame history with neutral past actions.
%
Training and checkpoint selection use the remaining upstream training recordings, split by recording (about 510
training and 90 validation recordings per task).

\subsection{Baselines, training and cost}\label{app:baselines}\label{app:training}
All learned baselines share the memory encoder, decoder, width, depth, optimiser and schedule of \ours{}, and differ
only in the output head and rollout.
%
AR is trained through its full 10-step rollout, and AR-TF, following \citet{zhou2025dinowm}, regresses absolute
next-step features with teacher forcing and is rolled out autoregressively at test time.
%
We evaluate the released V-JEPA 2-AC (ViT-g encoder, 300M predictor) zero-shot on exactly our test windows with its own
preprocessing, action convention and rollout, and fine-tune it on our training split with and without the head under an
identical budget.
%
\ours{} has 21.75M parameters against 21.70M for Direct, AR and AR-TF, and because \ours{} and Direct decode all $K$
steps in parallel, a 10-step forecast takes one decoder pass instead of ten.
%
One training run takes 0.55\,h for \ours{}, 0.47\,h for Direct, 0.91\,h for AR and 0.84\,h for AR-TF (median, two runs
sharing one H200).

\textbf{Optional action-contrastive loss.} A hinge with margin $m$ and stop-gradient $\operatorname{sg}$ compares the
error under the true future actions with the error under actions borrowed from another sample in the batch,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{act}} = \max\!\big(0,\; m - (\|\hat\Z(\tilde{\mathbf a})-\Z\|^2 - \operatorname{sg}\|\hat\Z(\mathbf a)-\Z\|^2)\big),
  \qquad \mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}}+\lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{act}} .
  \label{eq:contrastive}
\end{equation}
All forecasting and plug-in results use $\lambda{=}0$, like every baseline, and the loss enters only as an
ablation (\cref{sec:ablations}).

\subsection{Statistical procedures}\label{app:stats}
Each test episode contributes the mean of its windows (stride 2), and tables report the mean over training seeds.
%
For a paired comparison of methods A and B, we compute per-episode differences (averaged over seeds) and draw 10{,}000
bootstrap resamples of episodes, or of recording sessions for DROID, to form a percentile 95\% interval.
%
Table daggers use unadjusted intervals; for every dagger of \cref{tab:main} no resample crosses zero ($p<10^{-4}$),
so all remain significant after Holm correction over the three learned baselines of each dataset.
%
Training seeds per method are \droidSeeds{} for DROID, 2 for V-JEPA 2-AC and 1 for Open-H, Language-Table, IWS and the
ablations, and on DROID the full model's seed range is 0.2\% of its validation error.

\subsection{The future is a transport of observed content}\label{app:geometry}\label{app:analysis}
The \emph{oracle move} picks, for each moving patch, the observed candidate in its transport window closest to the true
future; it uses the future to choose and is therefore a model-free reference, not a predictor.
%
The share of its gain a model recovers is $(\text{err}_{\text{stay}}-\text{err}_{\text{model}})/
(\text{err}_{\text{stay}}-\text{err}_{\text{oracle}})$, computed over all moving patches of \geoWindows{} windows from
\geoEpisodes{} DROID test episodes.
%
\ours{} has lower error than both Direct and AR in every cell of horizon and motion decile (\cref{fig:geometry}a,b),
with the largest gain over Direct for moderate-to-large motion at long horizons and over AR on nearly static patches,
where the recursive rollout accumulates error on content that should not change.
%
It recovers a larger share of the oracle gain at every horizon (\cref{fig:geometry}c), and its advantage is not driven
by a few outliers, as it has lower error than Direct on \geoWinDirect\% of moving patches (\cref{fig:geometry}d).
%
Per episode, the relative gain shrinks with episode motion (Spearman $\rho=\wmRhoDirect$ between motion and the signed
relative difference to Direct), whereas the absolute error reduction grows with it, because the baselines' error rises
faster than the gain.

\subsection{Beyond feature error}\label{app:beyond}\label{app:flowwarp}\label{app:segments}
\textbf{Feature warping with optical flow.} We warp the last observed grid $\Z_0$ with RAFT \citep{teed2020raft} flow
pooled to the patch grid, on the same DROID test windows and metric as \cref{tab:main}.
%
\emph{Flow extrapolation}, in the style of F2M \citep{saric2020warp}, scales the flow between the last two observed
frames by $k$, whereas the \emph{oracle warp} uses the flow from the true future frame and thus upper-bounds a pure warp
of $\Z_0$; \ours{} improves on both, as its gate keeps static content, where estimated flow is noisy, and its
correction adds content that no warp of $\Z_0$ contains.

\textbf{Segmentation view.} Feature error does not show \emph{where} a forecast puts the arm, so we read a segmentation
out of every forecast.
%
Grounding DINO \citep{liu2024groundingdino} detects the arm or the instruments on the last observed frame and
SAM~2.1 \citep{ravi2025sam2} tracks the mask through the
true future, and we keep windows with a confident, stable track covering 3--128 of the 256 patches
(\segDroidWindows{} of \segDroidConsidered{} DROID and \segHamlynWindows{} of \segHamlynConsidered{} Hamlyn windows).
%
A forecast patch is foreground if it is more similar (cosine) to a foreground than to a background patch of $\Z_0$,
moving windows have below-median mask overlap between $t$ and $t{+}K$, and placement is the centroid distance.
%
On DROID, \ours{} has the highest arm IoU on moving windows, with paired intervals that exclude zero against Direct
and AR, and the lowest placement error, whereas on Hamlyn, where the instruments move less within ten steps, the three
predictors are close (\cref{fig:segments}).
