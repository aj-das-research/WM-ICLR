\section{Background}\label{app:background}
\subsection{The task and its uses}\label{app:setting}
An action-conditioned latent world model receives the last $H$ observed frames of a scene, encoded by a frozen visual
foundation model into grids of patch features, the actions applied between them, and a candidate sequence of $K$ future
actions.
%
It predicts the patch-feature grids of the next $K$ frames without seeing them, and it never generates pixels, as the
forecast lives in the feature space that downstream consumers read.
%
Such forecasts serve (i)~planning, where a model-predictive planner forecasts many candidate action sequences and
executes the one whose forecast is closest to a goal \citep{zhou2025dinowm,maes2026lewm}, (ii)~policy evaluation, where
a world model scores a policy's proposed actions before a real robot executes them, and (iii)~anticipation, where a
surgical system forecasts where instruments and tissue will be a few seconds ahead for assistance and safety monitoring.
%
All three need forecasts that are accurate many steps ahead, respond to the actions and hold on unseen scenes.

\Cref{fig:setting} shows the task on two held-out episodes, a Franka arm in a real kitchen (DROID) and a dVRK instrument
over a practice phantom (Open-H Hamlyn), chosen as the test episodes with the largest feature change.
%
The model receives three frames encoded into $16{\times}16$ grids of 384-D DINOv2 features, the actions between them
and ten future action blocks, and it predicts the feature grids 3.3\,s ahead.
%
Change concentrates on the robot and the objects it touches, as in these two windows a quarter of the patches carry more
than 40\% of the total change.
%
Most future features are thus already in the observed grid, displaced by motion, and only a small part is new content.

\subsection{Why current latent world models fall short}\label{app:why-short}
Current latent world models regress the complete feature grid at every future step, and most roll out recursively.
%
Our matched baselines isolate these two choices and expose three failure modes.
%
(i)~On static DROID patches, every learned regression predictor has higher error than copying the last frame, since a
regression head must output exactly zero change there.
%
(ii)~On moving patches, an additive head must write out the feature difference along the motion
(Proposition~\ref{prop:residual}).
%
(iii)~Recursion compounds error (Proposition~\ref{prop:compound}), and the teacher-forced AR-TF is worse than persistence
on DROID once rolled out.
%
\ours{} addresses all three, as it keeps static content through an exact identity path, moves observed content through
transport and decodes every horizon from observed features.

\subsection{Further related work}\label{app:related-extended}
Pixel-space generative world models learn action-controllable video simulators, from navigation (NWM;
\citealp{bar2025nwm}) to foundation models for physical AI (Cosmos; \citealp{nvidia2025cosmos}) and interactive
environments (Genie; \citealp{bruce2024genie}), and model-based reinforcement learning learns task-specific latent
dynamics jointly with rewards and policies \citep{hafner2019planet,hafner2025dreamerv3,hansen2024tdmpc2}.
%
These systems optimise sample fidelity or online return with their own encoders and data, so they do not share our
setting of a frozen encoder with targets in common feature coordinates, and we treat them as complementary.
%
Surgical world modelling is likewise driven by generative video models, such as Endora for endoscopy
video~\citep{li2024endora}, SurgWM with latent-action control~\citep{koju2025surgwm} and Cosmos-H-Surgical for policy
learning~\citep{he2025cosmoshsurgical}.
%
We instead use Open-H dVRK recordings~\citep{openh2026embodiment} to test kinematics-conditioned forecasting in feature
space, and for control we follow the latent planning evaluation of stable-worldmodel~\citep{maes2026stable}.

\section{Extended method}\label{app:method}
\subsection{Architecture}\label{app:impl}
The memory encoder attends over all $H\cdot N{=}768$ tokens of the three observed grids, each query-decoder layer
applies self-attention, cross-attention to memory and an MLP under AdaLN-zero, and actions are embedded by a 2-layer MLP
and a single-layer GRU of width $d$.
%
The transport uses key dimension 64 and a $7{\times}7$ window over $S{=}3$ source frames (147 candidates per query), and
the correction head applies LayerNorm, a zero-initialised linear map to $C$ and a per-channel scale initialised at 1.
%
\Cref{tab:hyper} lists all sizes and settings.

\subsection{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}
Throughout, $c=(\Z_{\le 0},\mathbf{a}_{<k})$ is the context, $\mathbf{e}_j$ the $j$-th standard basis vector, and
$\mathbf{z}_{0,j}$ the $j$-th row of $\Z_0$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:bayes}]
\emph{Optimal forecast.} Under squared error the optimal forecast is the conditional mean. Since $\Z_0$ is determined
by $c$ and the new content has zero conditional mean, the tower property gives
\begin{align}
\E[\Z_k\mid c]
  &= \E[\mathbf{P}_k\mid c]\,\Z_0+\E\big[\E[\bm{\varepsilon}_k\mid\mathbf{P}_k,c]\,\big|\,c\big]
   = \bar{\mathbf{P}}_k\Z_0 .
\end{align}
\emph{Structure of $\bar{\mathbf{P}}_k$.} Row $i$ of $\mathbf{P}_k$ is $\mathbf{e}_{\rho(i)}^{\top}$, so row $i$ of
$\bar{\mathbf{P}}_k$ is $\sum_{j}\Pr[\rho(i)=j\mid c]\,\mathbf{e}_j^{\top}$, a probability vector whose support lies in
$\mathcal{W}(i)$ whenever the support of $\rho(i)$ does.

\emph{Realisation by transport.} With $g=1$ and $\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{0}$, \cref{eq:output} gives
$\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{k,i}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{W}(i)}\pi_{k,i}(j)\,\mathbf{z}_{0,j}$. Let $\mathcal{S}_i$ be the support of
row $i$ of $\bar{\mathbf{P}}_k$, $u=|\mathcal{W}(i)\setminus\mathcal{S}_i|$, and choose logits
$\ell_j=\log\bar{P}_{k,ij}$ for $j\in\mathcal{S}_i$ and $\ell_j=-\Lambda$ otherwise. Since $\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}_i}\bar{P}_{k,ij}=1$,
\begin{align}
\pi_{k,i}(j)=\frac{\bar{P}_{k,ij}}{1+u e^{-\Lambda}}\;\;(j\in\mathcal{S}_i),\qquad
\pi_{k,i}(j)=\frac{e^{-\Lambda}}{1+u e^{-\Lambda}}\;\;(j\notin\mathcal{S}_i),
\end{align}
so the total-variation distance to row $i$ of $\bar{\mathbf{P}}_k$ is $2ue^{-\Lambda}/(1+ue^{-\Lambda})\le 2ue^{-\Lambda}$ and
\begin{align}
\big\|\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{k,i}-(\bar{\mathbf{P}}_k\Z_0)_i\big\|\;\le\;2u\,e^{-\Lambda}\max_{j\in\mathcal{W}(i)}\|\mathbf{z}_{0,j}\|
\;\xrightarrow[\Lambda\to\infty]{}\;0 .
\end{align}
With $S>1$ source frames the same argument holds with $\mathcal{W}(i)$ ranging over patches of all observed frames.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:residual}]
An additive head outputs $\Z_0+\mathbf{R}$. Matching $\mathbf{P}\Z_0$ requires
\begin{align}
\mathbf{R}=(\mathbf{P}-\mathbf{I})\Z_0,\qquad
\mathbf{R}_{i}=\mathbf{z}_{0,\rho(i)}-\mathbf{z}_{0,i},\qquad
\|\mathbf{R}\|_F^2=\sum_i\big\|\mathbf{z}_{0,\rho(i)}-\mathbf{z}_{0,i}\big\|^2 .
\end{align}
For \ours{}, take the one-hot transport $\pi_{k,i}=\mathbf{e}_{\rho(i)}$ (the limit $\Lambda\to\infty$ above, admissible since
$\rho(i)\in\mathcal{W}(i)$) and $g=1$. Then $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{k,i}=\mathbf{z}_{0,\rho(i)}=(\mathbf{P}\Z_0)_i$ for every $i$,
so the required correction is $\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{0}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:compound}]
Let the recursive rollout be $\hat{\Z}_j=f(\hat{\Z}_{j-1})$ with $\hat{\Z}_0=\Z_0$, the truth
$\Z_j=f(\Z_{j-1})+\bm{\eta}_j$ with $\|\bm{\eta}_j\|\le\delta$, and $e_j=\|\hat{\Z}_j-\Z_j\|$. By the triangle inequality
and the Lipschitz property,
\begin{align}
e_j\;\le\;\big\|f(\hat{\Z}_{j-1})-f(\Z_{j-1})\big\|+\|\bm{\eta}_j\|\;\le\;L\,e_{j-1}+\delta,\qquad e_0=0 .
\end{align}
By induction on $k$ (the case $k=1$ is $e_1\le\delta$; if $e_{k-1}\le\delta\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}L^j$ then
$e_k\le L\delta\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}L^j+\delta$),
\begin{align}
e_k\;\le\;\delta\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}L^j\;=\;
\begin{cases}\delta\,\dfrac{L^k-1}{L-1}, & L\neq 1,\\[4pt] k\,\delta, & L=1,\end{cases}
\end{align}
which grows geometrically for $L>1$. A direct predictor forms $\hat{\Z}_k$ from the observed inputs
$(\Z_{\le 0},\mathbf{a})$ alone, so its error at horizon $k$ is its per-horizon error $\delta_k$ and does not depend on
its predictions at other horizons.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Plug-in heads for published world models}\label{app:plugin}
For a published backbone with prediction $\mathbf{B}_k$, the head outputs $\hat\Z_k=(1-g_k)\mathbf{B}_k+g_k\mathbf{T}_k$,
where $\mathbf{T}_k$ is the local $7{\times}7$ soft transport, with identity bias, of \emph{observed} encoder tokens.
%
The gate $g_k=\sigma(\mathbf{w}^\top\h+b)$ starts at $\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{0}$ and $b=-4$, so training starts numerically
from the unmodified backbone, and nothing else in the backbone, loss or schedule changes.

\textbf{V-JEPA 2-AC.} Queries come from the predictor's normalised hidden state for each output block (1024-D), and
keys and values from the layer-normalised ViT-g/16 tokens ($16{\times}16$, 1408-D) of the last $S{=}3$ observed frames,
with a learned per-source key offset (0.16M parameters).
%
The fine-tuning objective is the released one (teacher-forced L1 plus rollout L1).
%
During rollouts the predictor consumes $\hat\Z_k$ as its own prediction, while $\mathbf{T}_k$ only ever reads observed
tokens.

\textbf{DINO-WM.} We subclass the official \texttt{ViTPredictor}, otherwise unmodified.
%
Queries come from the predictor's output token, and keys and values from its input token at the same slot on the
$14{\times}14$ DINOv2 patch grid ($S{=}1$); proprioceptive and action channels are left untouched.
%
In rollouts, the input token at that slot is DINO-WM's own previous prediction after the first step, so the head then
moves predicted rather than observed features, unlike \ours{} and the V-JEPA 2-AC head.
%
The extra parameters are drawn from a private random generator, so data order, initialisation and dropout of the base
predictor are identical in both arms.

\subsection{Baselines, training and cost}\label{app:baselines}\label{app:training}
All learned baselines share the memory encoder, decoder, width, depth, optimiser and schedule of \ours{}, and differ
only in the output head and rollout.
%
AR is trained through its full 10-step rollout, and AR-TF, following \citet{zhou2025dinowm}, regresses absolute
next-step features with teacher forcing and is rolled out autoregressively at test time.
%
We evaluate the released V-JEPA 2-AC (ViT-g encoder, 300M predictor) zero-shot on exactly our test windows with its own
preprocessing, action convention and rollout, and we fine-tune it on our training split with and without the head under
an identical budget; its training data likely include our test trajectories.
%
Validation MSE is computed every 2k steps, and the best checkpoint is tested (\cref{tab:hyper}).
%
\ours{} has 21.75M parameters against 21.70M for Direct, AR and AR-TF, and because \ours{} and Direct decode all $K$
steps in parallel, a 10-step forecast takes one decoder pass instead of ten.
%
One 8k-step training run takes 0.55\,h for \ours{}, 0.47\,h for Direct, 0.91\,h for AR and 0.84\,h for AR-TF (median,
two runs sharing one H200); the final analyses and planning runs used A100 GPUs.

\subsection{Closed-loop planning}\label{app:planning-results}\label{app:planning}
We evaluate closed-loop planning with the official LeWM and stable-worldmodel protocol (\cref{tab:plan-protocol}).
%
Every predictor, including the released LeWM checkpoint, is wrapped in one interface, which reproduces the native LeWM
planning costs exactly, and is driven by the same CEM
planner, goal sampling, budget and success criteria, so only the world model changes.
%
Among the matched predictors, which share a frozen DINOv2 encoder and a short training budget, PushT success ranges from
58.7\% (\ours{}) to 78.7\% (AR), and the released LeWM, trained end to end, reaches 88\% (\cref{fig:planning}).
%
Lower forecast error thus does not by itself raise planning success here.
%
Forecast error bounds planning regret only along directions that change the ranking of candidate plans.
%
If the planner scores $\hat c(\mathbf{a})=\|\hat{\mathbf{z}}_T(\mathbf{a})-\mathbf{z}_g\|^2$ with
$\|\hat{\mathbf{z}}_T-\mathbf{z}_T\|\le\varepsilon$ and $\|\mathbf{z}_T-\mathbf{z}_g\|\le R$, then
$|\hat c-c|\le2R\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2$, and a plan within $\eta$ of the best predicted cost has true cost at most
$\min_{\mathbf{a}}c(\mathbf{a})+4R\varepsilon+2\varepsilon^2+\eta$.

\subsection{Is the future a transport?}\label{app:geometry}\label{app:analysis}

with the largest advantage of \ours{} over the better baseline, so they are selected and not typical.

\subsection{Beyond feature error}\label{app:beyond}\label{app:flowwarp}\label{app:segments}
\textbf{Feature warping with optical flow.} We warp the last observed grid $\Z_0$ with RAFT \citep{teed2020raft} flow
pooled to the patch grid, on the same \geoWindows{} DROID test windows and metric as \cref{tab:main}.
%
\emph{Flow extrapolation}, in the style of F2M \citep{saric2020warp}, scales the flow between the last two observed
frames by $k$, whereas the \emph{oracle warp} uses the flow from the true future frame and thus upper-bounds a pure warp
of $\Z_0$.
%
Averaged over horizons, persistence, flow extrapolation, the oracle warp and \ours{} reach \flowPers{}, \flowExtrap{},
\flowOracle{} and \droidSWmse{}.
%
\ours{} beats even the oracle warp, as its gate keeps static content, where estimated flow is noisy, and its correction
adds content that no warp of $\Z_0$ contains.

\textbf{Segmentation view.} Feature error does not show \emph{where} a forecast puts the arm, so we read a segmentation
out of every forecast (\cref{fig:segments}).
%
Grounding DINO \citep{liu2024groundingdino} detects the arm (``a robot arm'') or the instruments (``a surgical
instrument'') on the last observed frame, and SAM~2.1 \citep{ravi2025sam2} segments it and tracks the mask through the
true future frames.
%
A window is kept if the detection is confident, the mask covers 3--128 of the 256 patches, the track is never lost and
the mask area never jumps by more than $3\times$, which keeps \segDroidWindows{} of \segDroidConsidered{} DROID windows
and \segHamlynWindows{} of \segHamlynConsidered{} Hamlyn windows.
%
Each forecast patch is labelled foreground if its most similar (cosine) foreground patch of $\Z_0$ is more similar than
its most similar background patch, a labeller fixed beforehand as the one with the highest IoU on the \emph{true}
future features (\segDroidOracleNn{} on DROID at $k{=}10$).
%
Moving windows are those whose masks at $t$ and $t{+}K$ overlap less than the median, and placement is the distance
between the centroids of the forecast and true masks.
%
On DROID, \ours{} has the highest IoU at every reported horizon, on all and on moving windows, with paired intervals
that exclude zero against both Direct and AR, and it has the lowest placement error on moving windows
(\segDroidPlaceSW{}\,px against \segDroidPlaceDi{} for Direct and \segDroidPlaceAR{} for AR).
%
On Hamlyn, where the instruments move less within ten steps, the three learned predictors are within half an IoU point
of each other.

\section{Discussion}\label{app:discussion}
\paragraph{When transport helps.} The gains are largest where content moves within the transport window and keeps its
appearance, as on DROID, where the advantage holds in every decile of motion; on the surgical tasks the instruments move
less within ten steps, and \ours{} is best on every task.

\paragraph{Towards fewer observed frames.} As a plug-in to DINO-WM on Wall, where the backbone conditions on a single
history frame, the head raises latent error by \dinowmWallErrInc\%, and restricting \ours{} itself to one source frame
raises its DROID error by \ablsone\%.
%
Content that no observed frame contains can only come from the correction, as the worst window in \cref{fig:rollouts}
shows, and motion larger than the window, such as camera ego-motion, lies outside the transport prior.
%
Longer observed histories and larger or adaptive windows are the natural extensions.

\paragraph{Uncertainty.} Under uncertain motion, the MSE-optimal forecast averages over plausible sources
(Proposition~\ref{prop:bayes}).
%
\ours{} averages observed features rather than regressing a mean, but it remains a deterministic model, and stochastic
feature forecasters \citep{boduljak2025vfmf,porcher2026flowwm} that target multimodal futures are complementary.
