\begin{abstract}
Latent world models forecast in the feature space of a frozen visual foundation model, but at every future step they
regenerate the entire feature grid, even though most of a manipulation scene only moves. We introduce \ours{}, a world
model that forecasts by \emph{moving what it has already seen}. For each prefix of future actions, \ours{} predicts a
local soft transport of observed patch features, a gate that decides where transport is used, and a correction for
content that transport cannot explain. We show that the optimal forecast under motion is itself such a transport, that
additive heads must instead learn the full feature contrast along the motion, and that anchoring every horizon to measured
features prevents compounding error. On held-out real robot video (DROID), \ours{} removes \droidSkill\% of persistence
error against \droidSkillAR\% for a matched recursive predictor, and it is the best predictor on seven surgical robot
tasks. As a plug-in head, it improves published world models: added to V-JEPA 2-AC and fine-tuned under an identical
budget, it lowers forecast error by \vjepaMSERed\% (\vjepaSkillFT\% $\to$ \vjepaSkillOurs\% skill); added to DINO-WM
with its official code, it changes latent prediction error by $-$\dinowmPushtErrRed\% (PushT) and $-$\dinowmWallErrRed\% (Wall). The learned transport field and gate are explicit, inspectable tensors that show where
the model predicts motion.
\end{abstract}
