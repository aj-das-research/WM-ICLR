\begin{abstract}
A world model forecasts how a scene responds to actions, so that an agent can evaluate plans before acting; forecasting
in the feature space of a frozen visual foundation model makes it compact and pixel-free.
Current latent world models such as DINO-WM and V-JEPA 2-AC regenerate the entire patch-feature grid at every future
step and usually feed their own predictions back. Yet in manipulation and surgical scenes most content persists and only
moves. Regenerating it corrupts the static background, blurs moving objects and lets errors compound over the rollout.
We introduce \ours{}, a world model that forecasts by \emph{moving what it has already seen}. For every future step it
predicts an action-conditioned soft local transport of measured patch features, a gate against the identity and a
correction for new content, decoding all horizons in parallel from observed features. Unlike flow warping it is
conditioned on actions and needs no flow supervision; unlike a regression head it is exact under motion; and as an output
head it attaches to published world models.
On held-out real-robot video (DROID, Language-Table) and seven surgical tasks, \ours{} lowers DROID error by
\droidVsDirect\% against a matched direct predictor and wins on \wmWinDirect\% of test episodes. As a plug-in it lowers
V-JEPA 2-AC's error by \vjepaMSERed\% and DINO-WM's by \dinowmPushtErrRed\% on PushT, but not on Wall (\dinowmWallErrInc\%
higher), whose single history frame leaves only predictions to transport.
\end{abstract}
