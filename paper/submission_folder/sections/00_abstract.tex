\begin{abstract}
A world model forecasts how a scene responds to actions. Forecasting in the feature space of a frozen visual foundation
model yields compact models that support planning without generating pixels. Current latent world models such as
DINO-WM and V-JEPA 2-AC regenerate the entire patch-feature grid at every future step and usually feed their own
predictions back as inputs. Yet in manipulation and surgical scenes most content persists and only moves. Regenerating it
wastes capacity, blurs moving objects, corrupts static background and lets errors compound over the rollout. We
introduce \ours{}, a world model that forecasts by \emph{moving what it has already seen}. For each prefix of future
actions it predicts a local soft transport of measured patch features, a gate that decides where transport applies, and
a correction for content that transport cannot explain. Every horizon is decoded directly from observed features, and
a simple motion model shows that the MSE-optimal forecast is itself such a transport. We compare \ours{} with
predictors that share its frozen encoder and architecture on held-out real robot video and seven surgical robot tasks,
and attach it as a plug-in head to two published world models. On held-out real robot video (DROID), \ours{} lowers forecast error
by \droidVsDirect\% relative to a matched direct predictor and by \droidVsAR\% relative to a matched recursive one; on
surgical tasks the gains are smaller (\hamlynVsDirect\% and \hamlynVsAR\%). Added to V-JEPA 2-AC under an identical
fine-tuning budget, it lowers forecast error by \vjepaMSERed\%; added to DINO-WM, it lowers latent error by
\dinowmPushtErrRed\% on PushT but raises it by \dinowmWallErrInc\% on Wall.
\end{abstract}
