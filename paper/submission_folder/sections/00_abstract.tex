\begin{abstract}
World models that forecast in the feature space of a frozen visual foundation model are fast
enough for planning, but they must regenerate every future patch from scratch, even though most
of a manipulation scene only moves. We introduce \ours{}, a latent world model that forecasts by
\emph{moving what it has already seen}. For each prefix of future actions, \ours{} predicts a local
soft transport of the observed patch features, a gate that decides where transport is used, and a
learned correction for content that transport cannot explain. Each forecast is computed directly
from measured features rather than from the model's own earlier predictions, so errors do not
compound through recursion, and the transport field is a readable estimate of predicted motion.
We evaluate on held-out real robot video (DROID), on surgical robot video with recorded kinematics
(Open-H-Embodiment, seven dVRK tasks), on single-image manipulation forecasting (IWS), and on
standard planning benchmarks (PushT, TwoRoom, Reacher). All comparisons use matched backbones
and frozen encoders (DINOv2, DINOv3). \pend{} % headline numbers are filled in only from completed runs
We also measure action sensitivity, a property that anchored predictors can lose, and show how an
action-contrastive objective recovers it for planning.
\end{abstract}
