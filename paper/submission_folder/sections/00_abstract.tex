\begin{abstract}
World models let an agent evaluate actions before executing them by forecasting how the scene will respond, and
forecasting in the patch-feature space of a frozen visual foundation model keeps them compact and free of pixel
generation.
%
Current latent world models, such as DINO-WM and V-JEPA 2-AC, regenerate the entire feature grid at every future step
and usually feed their own predictions back as inputs.
%
In manipulation and surgical scenes, however, most content persists and only moves, so regenerating it corrupts the
static background, forces the model to re-synthesise moving objects and lets errors compound over the rollout.
%
We introduce \ours{}, a latent world model that forecasts by \emph{moving what it has already seen}.
%
For every future step, \ours{} predicts an action-conditioned soft local transport of observed patch features, gates it
against keeping each feature in place, adds a correction for new content, and decodes all horizons in parallel from
measured features.
%
Unlike flow warping, the transport is conditioned on actions and learned without flow supervision, and because \ours{}
is an output head, it attaches to published world models.
%
We compare output heads under a shared frozen backbone on held-out real-robot video from DROID and Language-Table and on
seven surgical tasks from Open-H.
%
\ours{} has the lowest error on every dataset and lowers DROID feature error by \droidVsDirect\% relative to a matched
direct predictor; as a plug-in head, it lowers the error of the 1.3B-parameter V-JEPA 2-AC by \vjepaMSERed\% relative
to fine-tuning without the head.
\end{abstract}
