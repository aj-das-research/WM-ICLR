\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
\label{sec:setup}

We first compare \ours{} with predictors that differ only in the output head on held-out robot and surgical video
(\cref{sec:forecasting}), then attach its head to two published world models (\cref{sec:plugin}), test whether its
gains come from moving observed content (\cref{sec:analysis}), and remove one component at a time
(\cref{sec:ablations}).
%
Model selection uses validation splits only, and every test split is scored once.

\paragraph{Datasets.}
\textbf{DROID} \citep{khazatsky2024droid} contains episodes (recorded task demonstrations) of a real Franka
robot arm, split by recording session.
%
We train on the primary exterior camera and test zero-shot transfer to the second; one step is 0.33\,s, its 35-D
action block holds the five commanded gripper poses and openings of that step, and we forecast $K{=}10$ steps (3.3\,s).
%
\textbf{Open-H Hamlyn} \citep{openh2026embodiment} contains seven surgical training tasks, two on table-top phantoms (artificial practice models) and five on
ex-vivo porcine tissue, recorded with the dVRK (da Vinci Research Kit) robot, 80-D action blocks for its two arms and episode-disjoint splits per task.
%
\textbf{Language-Table} \citep{lynch2023interactive} shows an xArm robot arm pushing rigid blocks (a fixed Open-X
subset; \citealp{oneill2024openx}), and \textbf{IWS} \citep{wang2026iws,zhang2026rlawm} records real bimanual arms pushing a
T-block, handling a box and manipulating a rope, scored on the official validation handles, which come from recordings disjoint from the training recordings that also supply
our checkpoint-selection split.
%
For the plug-in study, we use the simulated PushT task (pushing a T-shaped block to a target pose) with the official
\textbf{DINO-WM} code.\footnote{\ghurl{https://github.com/gaoyuezhou/dino_wm}}
%
\Cref{tab:datasets} lists sizes, splits and licences, and \cref{fig:overview} maps all benchmarks, predictors and evaluations and defines the terms.

\paragraph{Baselines.} All learned predictors share a frozen DINOv2-S/14 encoder\footnote{\ghurl{https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov2}} ($16{\times}16$ patches, no pooling),
the same memory encoder and decoder (width 384, $L_e{=}L_d{=}4$, $\approx$22M parameters), optimiser, schedule, steps and
batch size, and differ only in the output head and rollout.
%
\textbf{Direct} replaces \cref{eq:output} with $\Z_0+\mathbf{W}_o\h_k$; it has the same cross-patch attention as
\ours{}, so it controls for spatial communication.
%
\textbf{AR} predicts a residual to the most recent grid and is rolled out recursively, with gradients through the
rollout.
%
\textbf{AR-TF} follows DINO-WM, as it is trained with teacher forcing to predict full next-step features and rolled out at
test time.
%
\textbf{Persistence} copies $\Z_0$, and \textbf{Linear} extrapolates the last feature difference, which multiplies frame-to-frame feature noise by up to $K$ and is therefore
far worse than persistence.
%
As published models, we use V-JEPA 2-AC\footnote{\ghurl{https://github.com/facebookresearch/vjepa2}} and DINO-WM with and without the \ours{} head.

\paragraph{Metrics and statistics.} The primary metric is standardised feature MSE on held-out test windows of $H{=}3$
observed and $K{=}10$ future steps ($K{=}12$ on IWS), averaged over horizons $k$ (steps ahead) and at the final step.
%
We also report \emph{skill}, the fraction of persistence error a model removes, which is scale-free and therefore
compares models in different feature spaces.
%
Episodes (recording sessions for DROID) are the statistical unit, and \cref{app:exp} gives all further details.
