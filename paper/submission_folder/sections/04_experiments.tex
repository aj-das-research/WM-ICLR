\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
\label{sec:setup}

We ask four questions. \textbf{(Q1)} Does transport improve held-out forecasting over matched predictors on real robot
and surgical video? \textbf{(Q2)} Does the \ours{} head improve \emph{published} world models? \textbf{(Q3)} Why does it
work? \textbf{(Q4)} Which components matter, and how does it plan? Selection uses validation splits
only; test splits are scored once.

\paragraph{Datasets} (\cref{fig:benchmarks}, \cref{tab:datasets,tab:tasks}).
\textbf{DROID} \citep{khazatsky2024droid}: real Franka episodes split by recording session; we train on the primary
exterior camera and test transfer to the second. One step is 0.33\,s with a 35-D Cartesian and gripper block; we
forecast $K{=}10$ steps (3.3\,s). \textbf{Open-H Hamlyn dVRK} \citep{openh2026embodiment}: seven surgical training
tasks with 80-D bimanual action blocks, episode-disjoint splits per task.

\textbf{Language-Table} \citep{lynch2023interactive}: an xArm pushing rigid blocks, from a pinned Open-X subset
\citep{oneill2024openx}. \textbf{Planning}: PushT with the official LeWM protocol \citep{maes2026lewm,maes2026stable}
(TwoRoom and Reacher are used to validate the harness), and PushT and Wall with the official DINO-WM code.

\paragraph{Baselines.} All learned predictors share a frozen DINOv2-S/14 encoder ($16{\times}16$ patches, no pooling),
the same memory encoder and decoder (width 384, $L_e{=}L_d{=}4$, $\approx$22M parameters), optimiser, schedule, steps and
batch size (\cref{app:impl}); they differ only in the output head and rollout. \textbf{Direct} replaces
\cref{eq:output} with $\Z_0+\mathbf{W}_o\h_k$; it has the same cross-patch attention, so it controls for spatial
communication. \textbf{AR} predicts a residual to the most recent grid and is rolled out recursively, with gradients
through the rollout. \textbf{AR-TF} follows DINO-WM: it is trained with teacher forcing to predict absolute features and
is rolled out at test time. \textbf{Persistence} copies $\Z_0$, and \textbf{Linear} extrapolates the last feature
difference. As published models we use V-JEPA 2-AC and DINO-WM, with and without the \ours{} head (\cref{sec:plugin}).

\paragraph{Metrics and protocol.} The primary metric is standardised feature MSE on held-out episodes, averaged over
horizons and at the final step. We also report \emph{skill}, the fraction of persistence error a model removes; being
scale-free, it compares models in different feature spaces. Episodes are the statistical unit (\cref{sec:stats}). Planning uses CEM with the feature distance to the goal grid as cost.
