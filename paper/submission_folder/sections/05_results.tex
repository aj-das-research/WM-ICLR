\gainDroidARTFOne\% at $k{=}1$ to \gainDroidARTFTen\% at $k{=}10$ on DROID as its errors compound

%

\subsection{Plug-in heads for open-source world models}
\label{sec:plugin}
\begin{wrapfigure}{r}{0.49\linewidth}
  \vspace{-14pt}\centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{plugin_plot.pdf}
  \captionsetup{skip=2pt}
  \caption{\textbf{\ours{} as a plug-in head.} Same model, data, schedule and loss with and without the head.
  V-JEPA 2-AC: DROID test, 2 seeds (dashed: released model, zero-shot); DINO-WM: PushT validation.}
  \label{fig:plugin}
  \vspace{-10pt}
\end{wrapfigure}
Because \ours{} is an output head, we attach it to two open-source world models without other changes.
%
The head blends the backbone's prediction $\mathbf{B}$ with transported observed features,
$\hat{\Z}=(1-g)\mathbf{B}+g\,\mathbf{T}$, with the gate initialised near zero so that training starts from the
unmodified model.
%
Each pair shares data, schedule, loss and seed.

\textbf{V-JEPA 2-AC.} The released model \citep{assran2025vjepa2} (ViT-g encoder, 300M predictor, trained on DROID)
removes \vjepaSkillZS\% of persistence error zero-shot on our test windows, \vjepaSkillFT\% after fine-tuning, and
\vjepaSkillOurs\% after fine-tuning \emph{with} the head under the identical budget (\cref{fig:plugin}).
%
The head thus lowers error by \vjepaMSERed\%, and by \vjepaMovingRed\% on moving patches, for 0.16M added
parameters.
%
The gate opens from 1.8\% to \vjepaGateTest\% at the selected checkpoint and \vjepaGateEnd\% by the end of fine-tuning,
and validation error is lower at every evaluation for both seeds, as \cref{app:plugin-dynamics} shows.
%
The model's training data likely overlap our test trajectories, which affects all three rows, so the head's gain is relative to the same starting point, data and budget.

\textbf{DINO-WM.} With the official code \citep{zhou2025dinowm}, the head lowers teacher-forced latent error, the one-step feature error given true
past features, by
\dinowmPushtErrRed\% on PushT and improves the decoded frames (SSIM and LPIPS).

\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{The gains concentrate on moving content.} The regression target of \cref{sec:theory} predicts gains on moving content
and exact copies of static content.
%
We split every test window into \emph{moving} patches, the 25\% with the largest true feature change (a property of the
data shared by all methods), and \emph{static} patches.
%
On moving patches, \ours{} has \droidMovingVsDirect\% lower error than Direct and \droidMovingVsAR\% lower than AR, and on
static patches it is the only learned model at or below copying, as regenerating the grid corrupts background that
should not change (\cref{tab:main}, right); the gains concentrate on the arm and gripper (\cref{fig:qualitative}a).
%
Per episode, \ours{} has lower error than Direct and AR on \wmWinDirect\% of the \wmEpisodes{} DROID test episodes long
enough for one window (two of 132 are shorter than 13 steps), its few losses lie in the less-moving half, and its
absolute gain grows with motion (\cref{fig:evidence}a,b).
%
The moving arm also lands in the right place: labelling each forecast patch by its most similar observed patch (cosine)
under the SAM~2.1 arm mask of the last observed frame, without any decoder, \ours{} has the highest arm IoU on moving
DROID windows, \segDroidMovGainDi{} points above Direct and \segDroidMovGainAR{} above AR (paired intervals exclude zero;
\cref{app:beyond}), and decoded forecasts show the arm and the instruments where they should be (\cref{fig:qualitative}b).

\paragraph{The trained model moves content through the transport.} Setting the gate to zero at test time, so that each forecast becomes
$\Z_0+\mathbf{r}$, raises the error by \koMoving\% on moving patches and \koHighGate\% on patches with a high learned
gate, but only by \koStatic\% on static patches, so content moves through the transport, not through the correction.
%
Swapping in another episode's future actions changes the transport field \steerRatio$\times$ more on moving than on
static patches, so the actions steer \emph{where} features are moved (\cref{fig:evidence}d).

\paragraph{The future is largely the present, moved.} As a model-free check of Proposition~\ref{prop:bayes}, we pick, for each moving
patch of \geoWindows{} DROID test windows, the observed feature in its transport window closest to the true future, an
\emph{oracle move}.
%
At $k{=}10$ it cuts the error of persistence by \geoOracleRed\%, whereas the same oracle with candidates from an
unrelated test episode is \geoNullWorse\% \emph{worse} than persistence (\geoNullSameLabWorse\% even from the same lab),
so the future is largely the episode's own observed content, displaced (\cref{fig:evidence}c).
%
\ours{} recovers \geoShareShift\% of this gain (Direct \geoShareDirect\%, AR \geoShareAR\%); as Direct differs only in the
transport, the gap between the shares is what transport adds, and \ours{} has lower error than Direct and AR in all 100
cells of 10 horizons by 10 motion deciles (\cref{app:geometry}).
%
The learned transport is also more than flow warping: \ours{} has \flowVsWarp\% lower error than warping $\Z_0$ with RAFT
optical flow (F2M-style) and
\flowVsOracle\% lower than warping with the true future flow, 18\% even without the correction term.

\paragraph{Statistical analysis.} \label{sec:stats} All intervals are paired bootstraps of per-episode differences (sessions
for DROID); every dagger in \cref{tab:main} survives Holm correction, and with one training seed (Open-H, Language-Table,
IWS) intervals reflect episode variance only (\cref{app:stats}).

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:ablations}
\Cref{fig:evidence}e changes one component at a time on DROID, scored on validation; the full model's seed range is
0.2\%.
%
\textbf{The gain comes from moving content.} Replacing the transport head by a direct regression head raises the error
by \abldirect\%, a $1{\times}1$ window that keeps the gate and correction but cannot move content by +\ablwone\%, and a
single source frame ($S{=}1$) by \ablsone\%.
%
Removing the correction costs +\ablnocorr\%, and removing the actions costs most (+\ablactfree\%), so the forecasts
follow the commanded motion.
%
Design alternatives stay within 1.2\%: a $5{\times}5$ window, a $\tanh$-bounded correction and an action-contrastive loss
change little, and a global window lowers error slightly (\ablglobal\%) but ranks actions less well (92.7 vs.\ 93.4\%)
and scores 768 instead of 147 candidates per patch.
