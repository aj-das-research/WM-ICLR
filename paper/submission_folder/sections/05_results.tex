\Cref{tab:main} compares \ours{} with predictors that share its backbone and differ only in how the forecast is formed.
On held-out DROID, \ours{} has the lowest error of all methods: \good{\droidVsDirect\%} lower than Direct,
\good{\droidVsAR\%} lower than the rollout-trained AR and \good{\droidVsARTF\%} lower than the DINO-WM-style AR-TF, whose

persistence error, against \droidSkillDirect\% for Direct and \droidSkillAR\% for AR. The gains transfer: zero-shot on
DROID's second exterior camera, which no model saw in training, \ours{} is \good{\camtwoVsDirect\%} below Direct and
\good{\camtwoVsAR\%} below AR; on Language-Table, where a robot pushes rigid blocks, \good{\ltVsDirect\%} and
\good{\ltVsAR\%}; and on the seven Open-H surgical tasks it is again best, \good{\hamlynVsDirect\%} below Direct and
\good{\hamlynVsAR\%} below AR. Paired 95\% intervals of the gain over the best baseline are [\droidCILo, \droidCIHi]\% on
DROID (resampling \droidTestSessions{} test sessions), [\ltCILo, \ltCIHi]\% on Language-Table and [\hamlynCILo,
\hamlynCIHi]\% on Open-H.

Per horizon (\cref{fig:horizon}), the gain over AR-TF grows from \gainDroidARTFOne\% at $k{=}1$ to
\good{\gainDroidARTFTen\%} at $k{=}10$ on DROID as its errors compound (Proposition~\ref{prop:compound}), and the gains over
Direct and AR stay positive at every horizon on DROID, Open-H and Language-Table. \ours{} is lowest at every horizon and on all seven

\subsection{Plug-in heads for published models (Q2)}
\label{sec:plugin}
\input{submission_folder/tables/plugin.tex}
Because \ours{} is an output head, we attach it to two published models without other changes: it blends the backbone's
prediction $\mathbf{P}$ with transported observed features, $\hat{\Z}=(1-g)\mathbf{P}+g\,\mathbf{T}$, with the gate
initialised near zero so training starts from the unmodified model. Each pair shares data, schedule, loss and seed.

\textbf{V-JEPA 2-AC.} The released model \citep{assran2025vjepa2} (ViT-g encoder, 300M predictor, trained on DROID)
removes \vjepaSkillZS\% of persistence error zero-shot on our test windows, \vjepaSkillFT\% after fine-tuning, and
\good{\vjepaSkillOurs\%} after fine-tuning \emph{with} the head under the identical budget (\cref{tab:plugin}):
\good{\vjepaMSERed\%} lower error and \vjepaMovingRed\% lower on moving patches for 0.16M added parameters. The gate
opens from 1.8\% to \vjepaGateTest\% at the selected checkpoint and \vjepaGateEnd\% by the end of fine-tuning, and
validation error is lower at every evaluation for both seeds (\cref{fig:vjepa-plugin}). Its training data likely overlap
our test trajectories, which affects the zero-shot row (\cref{app:baselines}).

\textbf{DINO-WM.} With the official code \citep{zhou2025dinowm}, the head lowers latent error by
\good{\dinowmPushtErrRed\%} on PushT. On Wall it raises latent error by \dinowmWallErrInc\%: DINO-WM conditions on a single
history frame ($S{=}1$), so after the first rollout step the head's ``observed'' token is DINO-WM's own prediction and
transport moves predicted rather than measured features. A single source frame also costs \ours{} \ablsone\% on DROID
(\cref{tab:ablations}). DINO-WM results are open-loop latent errors (\cref{app:plugin-dynamics}).

\label{sec:analysis}

  \label{fig:qualitative}

\paragraph{Where do the gains come from?} This analysis tests the target-size argument (Proposition~\ref{prop:residual}). We split every test window into \emph{moving} patches (the 25\% with the
largest true feature change, a property of the data shared by all methods) and \emph{static} patches
(\cref{tab:regions}). On moving patches, where transport moves observed content, \ours{} has \good{\droidMovingVsDirect\%}
lower error than Direct and \good{\droidMovingVsAR\%} lower than AR. On static patches, every other learned predictor is
\emph{worse than copying}, since regenerating the grid corrupts background that should not change; \ours{} is the only
learned model at or below copy, \good{\droidStaticVsDirect\%} below Direct and \good{\droidStaticVsAR\%} below AR. Across
all ten deciles of true motion, \ours{} has lower error than Direct (\good{+\decileMin\% to +\decileMax\%}), with the gains
concentrated on the arm and gripper (\cref{fig:qualitative}). Per episode, \ours{} has lower error than Direct on
\good{\wmWinDirect\%} and than AR on \good{\wmWinAR\%} of the \wmEpisodes{} test episodes; its few losses all lie in the
less-moving half (\cref{fig:winmap-main}; \cref{app:analysis}).

\paragraph{Reliance on transport.} The controlled evidence is the retrained Direct head, identical except for transport,
over which \ours{} lowers moving-patch error by \droidMovingVsDirect\%. At test time, setting the gate to zero (each
forecast becomes $\Z_0+\mathbf{r}$) raises the error by \koMoving\% on moving patches and by \koHighGate\% on patches with
a high learned gate, but only by \koStatic\% on static patches: the trained model moves content through transport, not
through the correction. Replacing the future actions with those of another episode changes the transport field
\good{\steerRatio$\times$} more on moving than on static patches, so the actions steer \emph{where} features are moved.

\paragraph{Is the future a transport?} As a model-free check of Proposition~\ref{prop:bayes}, on moving patches of
\geoWindows{} DROID test windows the single observed feature in the transport window closest to the true future (an
\emph{oracle move}) cuts the error of staying put by \geoOracleRed\% at $k{=}10$: half of the future is already in the
observed grid, displaced. \ours{} recovers \good{\geoShareShift\%} of this gain (Direct \geoShareDirect\%, AR
\geoShareAR\%), is better than Direct on \good{\geoWinDirect\%} of moving patches, and has lower error than Direct and AR
in \good{all 100} horizon-by-motion cells (\cref{fig:geometry,fig:anatomy}).

\paragraph{Beyond feature error.} The learned transport is more than flow warping: \ours{} has \good{\flowVsWarp\%} lower
error than RAFT-flow warping of $\Z_0$ (F2M-style) and \good{\flowVsOracle\%} lower than warping with the true future
flow (\cref{app:flowwarp}). Reading SAM~2.1 arm masks out of the forecasts, \ours{} has the highest arm IoU on moving
DROID windows, \good{\segDroidMovGainDi{}} points above Direct and \good{\segDroidMovGainAR{}} above AR, with paired CIs
excluding zero (\cref{app:segments}).

\paragraph{Statistical analysis.} \label{sec:stats} All comparisons are paired: we bootstrap per-episode differences
(recording sessions for DROID; 10{,}000 resamples, 95\% intervals), Holm-adjusted (\cref{app:stats}). Training seeds per
method: DROID \droidSeeds{}, Open-H \hamlynSeeds{}, Language-Table \ltSeeds{}, V-JEPA 2-AC 2, ablations 1; with one
seed, intervals reflect episode variance only.

\subsection{Ablations (Q4)}
\label{sec:ablations}
\input{submission_folder/tables/ablations.tex}
\Cref{tab:ablations} changes one component at a time on DROID, scored on validation. Replacing the transport head by a
direct regression head (Direct) raises validation error by \abldirect\% and lowers action-ranking accuracy, and restricting
transport to the last observed frame ($S{=}1$) raises it by \ablsone\%. The window rows separate moving from keeping:
$w{=}1$ keeps the gate and correction but cannot move content, and it loses the entire gain (+\ablwone\%, as much as
Direct), so the benefit comes from \emph{moving} observed content. A $5{\times}5$ window is within \ablwfive\%; a global window lowers error slightly (\ablglobal\%) but ranks actions less well (92.7 vs.\ 93.4\%) and
scores 768 instead of 147 candidates per patch, so the local window keeps the benefit of transport at a fifth of its
cost. A $\tanh$-bounded correction matches the learned scale (\abltanh\%), and removing the correction costs
+\ablnocorr\%. Removing the actions costs most (+\ablactfree\%): the forecasts depend on the
commanded motion, not only on the observed frames. An action-contrastive loss adds nothing (+\ablctr\%), so the transport
already uses the actions without it.
