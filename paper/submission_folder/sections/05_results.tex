\subsection{\ours{} as a plug-in for published world models (Q2)}
\label{sec:plugin}
\input{submission_folder/tables/plugin.tex}
Because \ours{} is an output head, we attach it to two published models without other changes: it blends the backbone's
prediction $\mathbf{P}$ with transported observed features, $\hat{\Z}=(1-g)\mathbf{P}+g\,\mathbf{T}$, with the gate
initialised near zero so training starts from the unmodified model. Each pair shares data, schedule, loss and seed.
\textbf{V-JEPA 2-AC.} The released model \citep{assran2025vjepa2} (ViT-g encoder, 300M predictor, trained on DROID)
removes \vjepaSkillZS\% of persistence error zero-shot on our test windows, \vjepaSkillFT\% after fine-tuning, and
\good{\vjepaSkillOurs\%} after fine-tuning \emph{with} the head under the identical budget (\cref{tab:plugin}):
\good{\vjepaMSERed\%} lower error and \vjepaMovingRed\% lower on moving patches for 0.16M added parameters. The gate
opens from 0.018 to about 0.55 within the first 250 steps, and validation error is lower at every evaluation for both
seeds (\cref{fig:vjepa-plugin}). V-JEPA 2-AC's training data likely include our test trajectories, which mainly
affects the zero-shot number.
\textbf{DINO-WM.} With the official code \citep{zhou2025dinowm}, the head lowers latent error by
\good{\dinowmPushtErrRed\%} on PushT (10.6\% lower over a 5-step rollout) but \emph{raises} it by \dinowmWallErrInc\% on
Wall, where DINO-WM conditions on a single history frame. Planning success changes from \dinowmPushtSuccBase\% to
\dinowmPushtSuccOurs\% (PushT) and \dinowmWallSuccBase\% to \dinowmWallSuccOurs\% (Wall); budgets are reduced and
identical for both arms (\cref{app:plugin}).

\subsection{Why transport helps (Q3)}
\label{sec:analysis}
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \figorpending{interpret.pdf}{5.0cm}{Where and why ShiftWM wins: win maps, causal knockout, motion deciles, action steering.}
  \caption{\textbf{Where and why \ours{} wins (held-out DROID, $k{=}10$).} (a) Transport arrows, true future, and Direct vs.\ \ours{} error on the same zoom. (b) Removing transport. (c) Gain per motion decile. (d) Action steering.}
  \label{fig:qualitative}
\end{figure}
\paragraph{Where do the gains come from?} This analysis tests the target-size argument (Proposition~\ref{prop:residual}). We split every test window into \emph{moving} patches (the 25\% with the
largest true feature change, a property of the data shared by all methods) and \emph{static} patches
(\cref{tab:regions}). On moving patches, transport predicts where content goes: \ours{} has \good{\droidMovingVsDirect\%}
lower error than Direct and \good{\droidMovingVsAR\%} lower than AR. On static patches, every other learned predictor is
\emph{worse than copying}, since regenerating the grid corrupts background that should not change; \ours{}'s identity
path keeps it intact, and it is the only learned model below persistence (\good{\droidStaticVsPers\%} lower;
\droidStaticVsAR\% lower than AR). Across all ten deciles of true motion, \ours{} has lower error than Direct
(\good{+\decileMin\% to +\decileMax\%}), with the gains concentrated on the arm and gripper (\cref{fig:qualitative}).

\paragraph{Causal role of transport.} This intervention tests the method itself. Setting the gate to zero at test time, so that each forecast is $\Z_0+\mathbf{r}$,
raises the error by \good{\koMoving\%} on moving patches and by \koHighGate\% on patches with a high learned gate, but only
by \koStatic\% on static patches: the transport pathway, not the correction, carries moving content. Replacing the
future actions with those of another episode changes the transport field \good{\steerRatio$\times$} more on moving than
on static patches, so the actions steer \emph{where} features are moved. Copying measured features also keeps
forecasts sharp: at $k{=}10$ \ours{} retains 80\% of the true spatial contrast, against 77\% for Direct and 74\% for AR
(\cref{fig:mechanism}). The transport field concentrates on the moving arm and gripper, while the background keeps
the identity path (\cref{fig:qualitative}a).

\paragraph{Is the future a transport?} As a model-free check of Proposition~\ref{prop:bayes}, on moving patches of
\geoWindows{} DROID test windows the single observed feature in the transport window closest to the true future (an
\emph{oracle move}) cuts the error of staying put by \geoOracleRed\% at $k{=}10$: half of the future is already in the
observed grid, displaced. \ours{} recovers \good{\geoShareShift\%} of this gain (Direct \geoShareDirect\%, AR
\geoShareAR\%), is better than Direct on \good{\geoWinDirect\%} of moving patches, and has lower error than Direct and AR
in \good{all 100} horizon-by-motion cells (\cref{fig:geometry,fig:anatomy}). Read out as a segmentation and compared
with SAM~2.1 masks of the true future (\cref{app:segments}), \ours{} places the moving DROID arm
\good{\segDroidPlaceRedAR{}\,px} closer than AR but only marginally closer than Direct (\segDroidPlaceRedDi{}\,px, CI
\segDroidPlaceRedDiCI{}); on Hamlyn all learned models are within a pixel.

\paragraph{Statistical analysis.} \label{sec:stats} All comparisons are paired: we bootstrap per-episode differences
(recording sessions for DROID; 10{,}000 resamples, 95\% intervals) and Holm-adjust primary contrasts (\cref{app:stats}).
Training seeds per method: DROID \droidSeeds{}, Hamlyn \hamlynSeeds{}, V-JEPA 2-AC 2, segmentation study 1. The DROID
and Hamlyn gains in \cref{tab:main} have intervals that exclude zero; with a single seed, intervals reflect episode
variance only.
