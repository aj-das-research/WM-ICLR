On held-out DROID, \ours{} has the lowest error of all methods: \good{\droidVsDirect\%} lower than Direct,

On Language-Table, where a robot pushes rigid blocks, \ours{} has \good{\ltVsDirect\%} lower error than Direct and \good{\ltVsAR\%} lower than AR (1 seed). Per horizon (\cref{fig:horizon,fig:horizon-abs}), the gain over the teacher-forced AR-TF grows from \gainDroidARTFOne\% at $k{=}1$ to
\good{\gainDroidARTFTen\%} at $k{=}10$ on DROID (Hamlyn \gainHamARTFOne\% to \gainHamARTFTen\%), as its errors compound

\subsection{\ours{} as a plug-in for published world models (Q2)}
\label{sec:plugin}
\input{submission_folder/tables/plugin.tex}
Because \ours{} is an output head, we attach it to two published models without other changes: it blends the backbone's
prediction $\mathbf{P}$ with transported observed features, $\hat{\Z}=(1-g)\mathbf{P}+g\,\mathbf{T}$, with the gate
initialised near zero so training starts from the unmodified model. Each pair shares data, schedule, loss and seed.

\textbf{V-JEPA 2-AC.} The released model \citep{assran2025vjepa2} (ViT-g encoder, 300M predictor, trained on DROID)
removes \vjepaSkillZS\% of persistence error zero-shot on our test windows, \vjepaSkillFT\% after fine-tuning, and
\good{\vjepaSkillOurs\%} after fine-tuning \emph{with} the head under the identical budget (\cref{tab:plugin}):
\good{\vjepaMSERed\%} lower error and \vjepaMovingRed\% lower on moving patches for 0.16M added parameters. The gate
opens from 1.8\% to \vjepaGateTest\% at the selected checkpoint and \vjepaGateEnd\% by the end of fine-tuning, and
validation error is lower at every evaluation for both seeds (\cref{fig:vjepa-plugin}). V-JEPA 2-AC's training data likely include our test trajectories, which mainly
affects the zero-shot number.

\textbf{DINO-WM.} With the official code \citep{zhou2025dinowm}, the head lowers latent error by
\good{\dinowmPushtErrRed\%} on PushT but \emph{raises} it by \dinowmWallErrInc\% on
Wall, where DINO-WM conditions on a single history frame ($S{=}1$): after the first rollout step the head's
``observed'' token is DINO-WM's own prediction, which we hypothesise (unproven) contributes to the regression. We report open-loop metrics only: one official MPC-CEM planning run takes about 7\,GPU-hours, and within our budget only the unmodified DINO-WM finished (\dinowmPushtSuccBase\% success on PushT); budgets are reduced and
identical for both arms (\cref{app:plugin,fig:vjepa-plugin}).

\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{Where do the gains come from?} This analysis tests the target-size argument (Proposition~\ref{prop:residual}). We split every test window into \emph{moving} patches (the 25\% with the
largest true feature change, a property of the data shared by all methods) and \emph{static} patches
(\cref{tab:regions}). On moving patches, transport predicts where content goes: \ours{} has \good{\droidMovingVsDirect\%}
lower error than Direct and \good{\droidMovingVsAR\%} lower than AR. On static patches, every other learned predictor is
\emph{worse than copying}, since regenerating the grid corrupts background that should not change; \ours{} keeps it
nearly intact, and it is the only learned model below persistence (\good{\droidStaticVsPers\%} lower;
\droidStaticVsAR\% lower than AR). Across all ten deciles of true motion, \ours{} has lower error than Direct
(\good{+\decileMin\% to +\decileMax\%}), with the gains concentrated on the arm and gripper (\cref{fig:qualitative}).
Per episode, \ours{} has lower error than Direct on \good{\wmWinDirect\%} and than AR on \good{\wmWinAR\%} of the
\wmEpisodes{} test episodes; its few losses all lie in the less-moving half (\cref{fig:winmap-main}; \cref{app:analysis}).

\paragraph{Reliance on transport.} Setting the gate to zero at test time, so that each forecast is $\Z_0+\mathbf{r}$,
raises the error by \koMoving\% on moving patches and by \koHighGate\% on patches with a high learned gate, but only
by \koStatic\% on static patches: the trained model relies on transport, not on the correction, for moving content. This
test-time intervention shows reliance, not necessity; the controlled evidence is the retrained Direct head without
transport, over which \ours{} lowers moving-patch error by \droidMovingVsDirect\%. Replacing the
future actions with those of another episode changes the transport field \good{\steerRatio$\times$} more on moving than
on static patches, so the actions steer \emph{where} features are moved.

\paragraph{Is the future a transport?} As a model-free check of Proposition~\ref{prop:bayes}, on moving patches of
\geoWindows{} DROID test windows the single observed feature in the transport window closest to the true future (an
\emph{oracle move}) cuts the error of staying put by \geoOracleRed\% at $k{=}10$: half of the future is already in the
observed grid, displaced. \ours{} recovers \good{\geoShareShift\%} of this gain (Direct \geoShareDirect\%, AR
\geoShareAR\%), is better than Direct on \good{\geoWinDirect\%} of moving patches, and has lower error than Direct and AR
in \good{all 100} horizon-by-motion cells (\cref{fig:geometry,fig:anatomy}). Against SAM~2.1 masks of the true
future (\cref{app:segments}), \ours{} places the DROID arm \segDroidPlaceRedAR{}\,px closer than AR but only
marginally closer than Direct (\segDroidPlaceRedDi{}\,px, CI \segDroidPlaceRedDiCI{}). Nor is it flow warping: \ours{} has \good{\flowVsWarp\%} lower error than RAFT-flow warping of $\Z_0$ (F2M-style) and \good{\flowVsOracle\%} lower than warping with the true future flow (\cref{app:flowwarp}).

\paragraph{Statistical analysis.} \label{sec:stats} All comparisons are paired: we bootstrap per-episode differences
(recording sessions for DROID; 10{,}000 resamples, 95\% intervals), Holm-adjusted (\cref{app:stats}). Seeds per
method: DROID \droidSeeds{}, Hamlyn \hamlynSeeds{}, Language-Table \ltSeeds{}, V-JEPA 2-AC 2; with one seed, intervals reflect episode variance only.
