\subsection{Plug-in heads for published models (Q2)}
\label{sec:plugin}
\input{submission_folder/tables/plugin.tex}
Because \ours{} is an output head, we attach it to two published models without other changes.
%
The head blends the backbone's prediction $\mathbf{B}$ with transported observed features,
$\hat{\Z}=(1-g)\mathbf{B}+g\,\mathbf{T}$, with the gate initialised near zero so that training starts from the
unmodified model.
%
Each pair shares data, schedule, loss and seed.

\textbf{V-JEPA 2-AC.} The released model \citep{assran2025vjepa2} (ViT-g encoder, 300M predictor, trained on DROID)
removes \vjepaSkillZS\% of persistence error zero-shot on our test windows, \vjepaSkillFT\% after fine-tuning, and
\vjepaSkillOurs\% after fine-tuning \emph{with} the head under the identical budget (\cref{tab:plugin}).
%
The head thus lowers error by \vjepaMSERed\%, and by \vjepaMovingRed\% on moving patches, for 0.16M added
parameters.
%
The gate opens from 1.8\% to \vjepaGateTest\% at the selected checkpoint and \vjepaGateEnd\% by the end of fine-tuning,
and validation error is lower at every evaluation for both seeds, as \cref{app:plugin-dynamics} shows.
%
The model's training data likely overlap our test trajectories, which affects only the zero-shot row.

\textbf{DINO-WM.} With the official code \citep{zhou2025dinowm}, the head lowers teacher-forced latent error, the one-step feature error given true
past features, by
\dinowmPushtErrRed\% on PushT.
%
On Wall it raises latent error by \dinowmWallErrInc\%, as DINO-WM conditions on a single history frame ($S{=}1$), which
leaves the head one observed frame to move and, after the first rollout step, only DINO-WM's own predictions.

\subsection{Why transport helps (Q3)}
\label{sec:analysis}
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \figorpending{interpret.pdf}{5.0cm}{Where and why ShiftWM wins: win maps, causal knockout, motion deciles, action steering.}
  \caption{\textbf{Where and why \ours{} wins (held-out DROID, $k{=}10$).} (a) Transport arrows, true future, and Direct vs.\ \ours{} error on the same zoom (selected windows). (b) Removing transport. (c) Gain per motion decile. (d) Action steering.}
  \label{fig:qualitative}
\end{figure}
\paragraph{Where do the gains come from?} Proposition~\ref{prop:residual} predicts gains on moving content and exact
copies of static content.
%
We split every test window into \emph{moving} patches, the 25\% with the largest true feature change (a property of the
data shared by all methods), and \emph{static} patches.
%
On moving patches, \ours{} has \droidMovingVsDirect\% lower error than Direct and \droidMovingVsAR\% lower
than AR.
%
On static patches, every other learned predictor is \emph{worse than copying}, as regenerating the grid corrupts
background that should not change, whereas \ours{} is the only learned model at or below copy,
\droidStaticVsDirect\% below Direct and \droidStaticVsAR\% below AR.
%
Across all ten deciles of true motion, \ours{} has lower error than Direct (+\decileMin\% to +\decileMax\%),
with the gains concentrated on the arm and gripper (\cref{fig:qualitative}a,c).
%
Per episode, \ours{} has lower error than Direct on \wmWinDirect\% and than AR on \wmWinAR\% of the
\wmEpisodes{} test episodes, and its few losses all lie in the less-moving half (\cref{fig:winmap-main}).

  \vspace{-1.3em}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{winmap_compact.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Per-episode gain} (DROID test, mean over $k$; below 0: \ours{} better; circles: losses).}
  \label{fig:winmap-main}
  \vspace{-1.2em}
\end{wrapfigure}
\paragraph{Reliance on transport.} The controlled evidence is the retrained Direct head, identical except for
transport, over which \ours{} lowers moving-patch error by \droidMovingVsDirect\%.
%
At test time, setting the gate to zero, so that each forecast becomes $\Z_0+\mathbf{r}$, raises the error by
\koMoving\% on moving patches and by \koHighGate\% on patches with a high learned gate, but only by \koStatic\% on
static patches (\cref{fig:qualitative}b).
%
The trained model therefore moves content through the transport, not through the correction.
%
Swapping in another episode's future actions changes the transport field, the expected source offset of each patch, \steerRatio$\times$ more
on moving than on static patches (\cref{fig:qualitative}d), so the actions steer \emph{where} features are moved.

\paragraph{Is the future a transport?} As a model-free check of Proposition~\ref{prop:bayes}, we pick, for each moving
patch of \geoWindows{} DROID test windows, the observed feature in its transport window closest to the true future, an
\emph{oracle move}.
%
At $k{=}10$ the oracle move cuts the error of persistence by \geoOracleRed\%, so half of the future is already in the
observed grid, displaced.
%
\ours{} recovers \geoShareShift\% of this gain (Direct \geoShareDirect\%, AR \geoShareAR\%), is better than
Direct on \geoWinDirect\% of moving patches, and has lower error than Direct and AR in all 100
cells of 10 horizons by 10 motion deciles, as \cref{app:geometry} details.

\paragraph{Beyond feature error.} The learned transport is more than flow warping, as \ours{} has \flowVsWarp\%
lower error than warping $\Z_0$ with RAFT optical flow (F2M-style) and \flowVsOracle\% lower than warping with the true
future flow.
%
Reading arm masks of the SAM~2.1 segmentation model out of the forecasts, \ours{} has the highest arm IoU on moving DROID windows,
\segDroidMovGainDi{} points above Direct and \segDroidMovGainAR{} above AR, with paired intervals that
exclude zero; \cref{app:beyond} gives both protocols.

\paragraph{Statistical analysis.} \label{sec:stats} All comparisons are paired, as we bootstrap per-episode
differences (recording sessions for DROID; 10{,}000 resamples, 95\% intervals) and Holm-adjust them for multiple comparisons.
%
Training seeds per method are \droidSeeds{} for DROID, \hamlynSeeds{} for Open-H, \ltSeeds{} for Language-Table, 2 for
V-JEPA 2-AC and 1 for ablations; with one seed, intervals reflect episode variance only.

\subsection{Ablations (Q4)}
\label{sec:ablations}
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{ablations.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Ablations on DROID validation} (seed 0; selection on validation only), sorted by $\Delta$, the change of
  validation MSE against the full model (positive = worse); action-ranking accuracy and absolute MSE on the right.}
  \label{fig:ablations}
\end{figure}
\Cref{fig:ablations} changes one component at a time on DROID, scored on validation.
%
Replacing the transport head by a direct regression head (Direct) raises validation error by \abldirect\% and lowers
action-ranking accuracy, and restricting transport to the last observed frame ($S{=}1$) raises it by \ablsone\%.
%
\textbf{The gain comes from moving content.} A $1{\times}1$ transport window ($w{=}1$) keeps the gate and correction but cannot
move content, and its error rises by +\ablwone\%, as much as for Direct.
%
A $5{\times}5$ window is within \ablwfive\%, and a global window lowers error slightly (\ablglobal\%) but ranks actions
less well (92.7 vs.\ 93.4\%) and scores 768 instead of 147 candidates per patch, so the local window keeps the benefit
of transport at a fifth of the cost.
%
A $\tanh$-bounded correction matches the learned scale (\abltanh\%), and removing the correction costs +\ablnocorr\%.
%
Removing the actions costs most (+\ablactfree\%), so the forecasts depend on the commanded motion and not only on the
observed frames, and an action-contrastive loss adds nothing (+\ablctr\%), as the transport already uses the actions.
