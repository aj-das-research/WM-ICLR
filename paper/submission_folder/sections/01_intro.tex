\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A world model predicts how observations change in response to actions, so that an agent can evaluate candidate actions
before acting. Recent work forecasts in the patch-feature space of a frozen self-supervised encoder such as DINOv2 or
V-JEPA~2, giving compact models that plan without generating pixels
\citep{zhou2025dinowm,assran2025vjepa2,maes2026lewm,sobal2025pldm}. For robot manipulation and surgical robotics, such a
model must forecast several seconds ahead, respond to the commanded actions and hold on scenes it has not seen.

Current latent world models are \emph{generative in feature space}: at every future step they regress the complete
grid of patch features, and most feed their own predictions back as inputs. In manipulation and surgical video,
however, most of the scene persists: the table, tissue, tools and robot move but are rarely created or destroyed.
Regenerating persistent content causes three failures. Static background must be rewritten exactly, yet on held-out
robot video every matched regression predictor we train is worse than copying the last frame on static patches. Moving
content must be synthesised as a displacement in feature space, which blurs object boundaries. Recursive rollouts
compound both errors: a DINO-WM-style predictor trained with teacher forcing is worse than persistence once rolled out.
Pixel and feature forecasters exploit motion by warping \citep{finn2016advection,liu2017voxelflow,saric2020warp}, but
latent world models for planning do not, although much of the future is already observed: on moving patches of held-out
DROID, copying the single best observed feature from a local window removes \geoOracleRed\% of the error of staying put.

We introduce \ours{}, a latent world model that forecasts by \emph{moving what it has already seen}
(\cref{fig:teaser,fig:method}): for every future step, each patch selects where its content comes from among nearby
measured features, conditioned on the actions, and a gate and a small correction handle what does not
move. Every horizon is decoded in parallel from observed features, so errors never feed back, and because \ours{} is an
output head, the same move-and-correct forecast attaches to published world models.

We compare \ours{} with predictors that share its frozen encoder and architecture and differ only in the output head,
on held-out real-robot video (DROID, with a zero-shot second camera, and Language-Table) and seven Open-H surgical tasks,
with paired statistics over held-out episodes and recording sessions. We attach it to V-JEPA 2-AC and DINO-WM, and test where the gains arise, whether the model relies on transport and whether
the future is a transport (\cref{fig:benchmarks}).

In this paper, our major contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=1pt,parsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Forecasting by transport.} We introduce \ours{}, a latent world model that moves observed patch features
  with an action-conditioned soft local transport, gated against the identity and corrected for new content,
  and decodes every horizon in parallel with $<\!0.3\%$ added parameters; under a motion-plus-innovation model, this
  transport is the MSE-optimal forecast (\cref{sec:method}).
  \item \textbf{A plug-in head for published world models.} Added to the 1.3B-parameter V-JEPA 2-AC, it lowers DROID
  error by \vjepaMSERed\% (\vjepaMovingRed\% on moving patches) with 0.16M parameters; on DINO-WM PushT, by
  \dinowmPushtErrRed\% (\cref{sec:plugin}).
  \item \textbf{A matched evaluation on real-robot and surgical video.} Output heads are compared all else equal
  on session-disjoint DROID with a zero-shot second camera, Language-Table and seven Open-H surgical tasks
  (1{,}126, 2{,}589 and 1{,}013 episodes) with paired bootstrap tests (\cref{sec:experiments}).
  \item \textbf{Consistent gains, and why.} On DROID, error is \droidVsDirect\%, \droidVsAR\% and \droidVsARTF\% below
  Direct, AR and AR-TF, with wins on \wmWinDirect\% of test episodes; the gain holds zero-shot on an unseen camera
  (\camtwoVsDirect\%), on Language-Table (\ltVsDirect\%) and on all seven surgical tasks. Half of the moving future is
  displaced observed content, and \ours{} recovers \geoShareShift\% of that gain (\cref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}
