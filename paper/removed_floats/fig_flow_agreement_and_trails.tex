\paragraph{Transport versus optical flow.} \Cref{fig:flow} compares the expected source displacement of each patch with
RAFT optical flow \citep{teed2020raft} pooled to the patch grid. On DROID (three seeds, seed 0 quoted; the others
agree within 0.03), the transport points in the direction of the backward flow (cosine 0.42, 0.33, 0.30 at
$k{=}1,5,10$) but moves too little: its end-point error, 0.81, 1.43 and 1.56 patches, is only slightly below that of
assuming no motion (0.92, 1.56, 1.65). On Hamlyn the cosine is 0.19--0.35 and the error exceeds the no-motion
reference at $k{=}1$ (1.03 vs.\ 0.99 patches). The transport is therefore not an optical-flow estimator
(\cref{fig:trails}).

\paragraph{Transport trails.} \Cref{fig:trails} follows the transport over the horizon. For each query patch $i$ and horizon $k$, the trail point is $i+\mathbf{o}_k(i)$, with
$\mathbf{o}_k(i)$ the $\pi$-weighted mean offset over the window and the three source frames; the true trail is
$i+\mathbf{b}_k(i)$, with $\mathbf{b}_k$ the RAFT flow from frame $t_0{+}k$ back to $t_0$ pooled to the grid (the
protocol of \cref{fig:flow}; no pooled flow was cached, so it is recomputed). Moving patches have $|\mathbf{b}_{10}|>0.5$
patch (\trPatches{} patches, \trWindows{} windows, \trEpisodes{} episodes).

The trails point the right way (cosine
\trCosTen{} at $k{=}10$) but are much shorter than the true motion: the endpoint error (\trEpeTen{} patches at $k{=}10$)
is above the zero-motion baseline up to $k{=}8$ and only slightly below it at $k{=}10$ (\trZeroTen{}), and the
$7{\times}7$ window explains little of the gap (reach limit \trReachTen{}). The transport is therefore not an optical-flow
estimator: it fetches matching features mostly from nearby positions and earlier frames.
