Visual world models must predict the effects of actions when both a scene's appearance and its behavior change. We present ShiftWM, a family of compact models that uses observed images and actions to improve future predictions. One variant learns separate summaries of appearance and dynamics through paired training interventions. A spatial variant keeps the last observed visual state as an anchor, mixes its features according to the action sequence, and adds bounded corrections. This design preserves observed information while learning how it changes. Experiments show lower forecast error on unseen appearance–dynamics combinations in simulated pushing and reaching, with additional forecasting gains on recorded robot video. In a matched DROID development study, the spatial variant reduces ten-step feature error by 5.3% relative to autoregression. We release trained checkpoints, evaluation code and visual diagnostics to support reproducible research on compact world models.
