Visual world models must predict how a scene changes after an action while preserving useful information from real observations. We propose ShiftWM, a compact predictor that keeps the last observed visual feature grid as a fixed reference. For each future action prefix, it mixes features from this grid and adds a bounded correction. This gives every forecast a direct path to measured visual evidence. In a matched development study on recorded DROID robot video, ShiftWM reduces ten-step endpoint feature error by 5.3% relative to autoregression. Component experiments support action-conditioned mixing as the clearest contributor to the improvement. A separate single-image study finds that removing the correction bound improves forecasting over matched autoregression on reserved recordings from three manipulation tasks. A separate matched simulator study finds no conclusive PushT control improvement and substantially lower Reacher success than autoregression, limiting the contribution to forecasting. We provide trained checkpoints, reproducible evaluation code and visual diagnostics.
