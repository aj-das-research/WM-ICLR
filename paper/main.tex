\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Research draft; mechanism controls and baseline coverage remain incomplete.}
A visual world model may fail because its observations changed, its physical
dynamics changed, or both. When appearance and dynamics are correlated in
training, familiar changes can be difficult to compose at deployment.
We study \emph{ShiftWM}, a compact action-conditioned world model with
separately inferred observation and dynamics contexts. An observation
context calibrates image features, while a dynamics context uses calibrated
history and executed actions to condition future prediction. Training uses
paired appearance interventions and fixed canonical targets; deployment
requires only available observations and actions. We evaluate on controlled
appearance--dynamics combinations in visual pushing and reaching, comparing
against matched framewise, shared-context, and unpaired-context controls on
the same LeWM backbone. Across three training seeds, ShiftWM lowers held-out
forecast error relative to framewise calibration by 3.7\% on PushT and
26.8\% on Reacher, but increases Reacher extrapolation error. The completed
three-seed planning comparisons show mixed point estimates and do not
establish an advantage over matched controls. A development-only
goal-calibration intervention further shows why forecast and planning
quality must be assessed separately. The current evidence supports a
predictive benefit under held-out composition, not factor identification
or broad transfer. On real DROID recordings, a separately frozen test on
65 additional episodes confirms a 0.74\% short-horizon error reduction
against equally calibrated Framewise after applying the same train-fitted
residual calibration to both methods; long-horizon differences remain
inconclusive.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
An agent planning with a visual world model must predict the consequences of
its actions under the conditions it encounters. A camera exposure change
alters the observation process; a friction change alters physical response.
Either can increase prediction error, but correcting the wrong part of the
model can distort useful knowledge. This distinction becomes particularly
important when appearance and dynamics are correlated in training: a model
may use color as a shortcut for action response.

Compact latent models let us investigate this question without training a
large video generator. LeWorldModel (LeWM) provides a small action-conditioned
architecture and planning code \citep{maes2026lewm}. Building on that released
system, we ask: \emph{can intervention-paired learning of separate observation
and dynamics contexts improve planning on unseen combinations of otherwise
familiar conditions?}

ShiftWM (ours) first maps observed features into a fixed pretrained coordinate
system. It then infers dynamics context from corrected histories and executed
actions. A standard model-predictive controller scores candidate futures
against a goal image in the same coordinates. No simulator parameter or
future observation enters context inference at deployment.

The study makes three concrete contributions: a controlled composition
protocol with matched information access; an intervention-paired,
two-context implementation built on a released compact world model; and an
analysis separating forecast accuracy, goal calibration, and closed-loop
planning. Our main comparison is Table~\ref{tab:measured-primary};
Table~\ref{tab:methods} specifies exactly what each method changes.
The strongest completed result against the fixed Framewise calibration
reference is \textbf{26.8\% lower held-out Reacher forecast error}, alongside
3.7\% lower PushT error. The completed planning campaign has mixed outcomes,
including lower held-out success point estimates than Framewise calibration
in both environments. Context-specific information use has not been
established. We therefore present the method
as a tested hypothesis with explicit controls, not an identified physical
representation or a general adaptation solution.

To examine recorded observations beyond simulation, we additionally study
a support-conditioned variant on 1,126 real DROID robot episodes, using
session-disjoint splits and a held-out camera view
(Appendix~\ref{app:real-video}). This variant uses frozen DINOv2 targets
without paired canonical renders. Its offline forecasting results are
reported separately from the controlled composition and planning experiments.
A subsequent comparison freezes all methods before inspecting 65 additional
recordings from 52 new sessions (Appendix~\ref{app:fresh-real-video}). With
identical train-fitted residual calibration, ShiftWM reduces the prespecified
five-block endpoint error by 0.742\% versus Framewise; its paired interval
excludes zero. This provides independent evidence for a small forecasting
benefit, while longer-horizon comparisons remain inconclusive.

\section{Related work}
\paragraph{Predictive models and context.}
We reuse LeWM's encoder, action embedding, causal predictor, and planning
interface \citep{maes2026lewm}, with \texttt{stable-worldmodel} infrastructure
\citep{maes2026stable}; compactness is an upstream property.
ContextWM separates scene context from dynamics \citep{wu2023contextwm},
ICWM infers system information from interaction histories \citep{wang2026icwm},
and work on habit, physics, and nuisance separates action selection,
transition, and observation effects \citep{hang2026habit}.
In surgical simulation, SWoMo separates rule-based tool motion from
diffusion-based appearance generation \citep{sivakumar2026swomo}.
Our scope is support-conditioned latent prediction and matched-budget control;
separation of appearance and dynamics itself is not a new claim.
Our candidate distinction is intervention-paired training and held-out
composition when both appearance and physical response vary. Two context
vectors alone do not establish novelty.

\paragraph{Adaptation and physical conditioning.}
MoVie adapts visual model-based policies across views \citep{yang2023movie};
AdaJEPA updates a latent model in the control loop \citep{wang2026adajepa}.
SG-JEPA studies supplied physical conditioning and multi-step latent
consistency \citep{liu2026sgjepa}. ShiftWM uses inferred context without
online gradients or true physical parameters. Our separate AdaJEPA
reproduction retains its official checkpoint, proprioceptive inputs and
planning budget; it is not a matched baseline for ShiftWM. MoVie and SG-JEPA
remain unreproduced comparisons.
Identifiability depends on representation assumptions and action excitation
\citep{zhang2026identifiability}; our prediction loss does not uniquely
identify physical quantities. A final literature audit and stronger direct
comparisons remain necessary to substantiate a novelty claim.

\paragraph{Decision geometry.}
DA-LeWM distinguishes informative representations from planning-cost alignment
and studies predicted--executed plan rankings \citep{wang2026dalewm}.
Monotone cost ranking combines frozen visual features, recursive forecasting
and action-conditioned cost shaping \citep{chahe2026monotone}.
Our branch-ranking diagnostic acknowledges this precedent; neither that
diagnostic nor a larger feature-calibration range is claimed as a new method.

\section{Problem and information access}
\label{sec:problem}
Let $x_t$ be hidden physical state, $a_t$ an executed action, $p$ a dynamics
setting, and $v$ an observation setting. Within a stationary segment,
\begin{equation}
 x_{t+1}=F_p(x_t,a_t,\epsilon_t),\qquad o_t=R_v(x_t,\eta_t).
 \label{eq:environment}
\end{equation}
The deployed agent receives images and actions, not $x_t,p,v$.
Training covers selected pairs $(v,p)$. Compositional testing holds out a
pair while retaining each factor individually in training; extrapolation
to new values is a separate evaluation. Appearance transforms preserve the
action coordinate system. Camera geometry changes and noninvertible
occlusion require different protocols.

An earlier support segment $S$ contains $H$ observations and $H-1$ actions;
later query images $Q$ are disjoint from support. At deployment, support
is real past history, and the controller receives a goal image. Imagined
futures never become evidence for context inference. Complete trajectory
families, including appearance-paired renders, stay within one data split.

\paragraph{Training supervision and fairness.}
A canonical render depicts the \emph{same physical state} under a reference
appearance; changing dynamics requires a new rollout. All trained controls
receive the same trajectories, canonical targets, training schedule, and
validation selection. ShiftWM additionally uses pairing identities for
context consistency. Shared versus unpaired contexts tests architecture;
unpaired contexts versus ShiftWM tests the extra consistency supervision.
A comparison against the frozen model alone cannot attribute a gain to
factorization.

\section{ShiftWM: observation and dynamics contexts}
\label{sec:method}
The visual encoder and projector $E_0$ are frozen. We train context networks,
adapters, the action encoder, predictor, and prediction projector offline.
Weights remain fixed online. Figure~\ref{fig:method} foregrounds the shared
observation calibration (A), corrected-transition dynamics inference (B), and
additional paired supervision (C); the LeWM backbone and CEM planner are
reused. Appendix~\ref{sec:implementation} gives
dimensions, initialization, and parameter counts.

\subsection{Context inference and prediction}
For $u_t=E_0(o_t)\in\R^d$, the observation pathway computes
\begin{equation}
 c_o=C_o(u_S),\qquad z_t=A_o(u_t,c_o).
 \label{eq:obs}
\end{equation}
The residual adapter $A_o$ calibrates shifted features to reference
coordinates. Dynamics context uses chronological corrected support and
executed actions:
\begin{align}
 c_d&=C_d\big((z_i,a_i,z_{i+1})_{i\in S}\big),\nonumber\\
 \widetilde a_t&=A_d(E_a(a_t),c_d),\qquad
 \hat z_{t+1}=P_\theta(z_{t-L+1:t},\widetilde a_{t-L+1:t}).
 \label{eq:prediction}
\end{align}
Here $A_d$ is an action-embedding adapter and $L$ is predictor history.
Correcting support before dynamics inference is an inductive bias, not
proof of independence; uninformative actions can leave dynamics ambiguous.
Contexts are inferred once per replan and held fixed over candidate rollouts.

\subsection{Learning in fixed coordinates}
For a canonical training render $o_t^*=R_{v_0}(x_t)$, the target is
$z_t^*=\sg(E_0(o_t^*))$. The prediction loss $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pred}}$
is teacher-forced one-step query MSE, and $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{align}}$
is corrected-feature MSE to the same frozen reference. Appearance variants
of the same trajectory encourage equal dynamics contexts through
$\mathcal L_d$. Different batch examples sharing an appearance label
encourage equal observation contexts through $\mathcal L_o$.
The objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pred}}
 +\lambda_{\mathrm{align}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{align}}
 +\lambda_d\mathcal L_d+\lambda_o\mathcal L_o,
 \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with weights $(1,0.1,0.01)$. Unpaired contexts disable the two consistency
terms. Targets have fixed coordinates, but the contexts themselves may
collapse or rescale with their adapters. Constant-context and shuffled-context
controls are therefore required before interpreting the contexts as useful
factor-specific information. Appendix~\ref{sec:loss-details} states the
exact index sets and batch-pairing rules, including the mismatch between
one-step training and recursive evaluation.

\subsection{Planning}
The goal embedding is $z_g=A_o(E_0(o_g),c_o)$, assuming the goal shares
the current observation setting. A common CEM/MPC solver minimizes
\begin{equation}
 J(a_{t:t+K-1})=\operatorname{mse}(\hat z_{t+K},z_g),
 \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
executes the first action chunk, and replans from new real observations.
All methods share candidate count, iterations, action bounds, horizon,
and paid interaction budget. Different-view goals and online gradient
adaptation are outside the completed primary comparison.

\section{Experiments}

\paragraph{Does composition improve planning?}
The complete matched comparisons show mixed effects. Relative to Framewise
calibration, held-out raw-success differences are $-1.04$ percentage points
on PushT (conditional 95\% interval $[-3.65,1.56]$) and $-2.08$ on Reacher

These intervals condition on the three observed training seeds.

Positive held-out point estimates occur against Shared context ($+0.52$
points on PushT and $+4.17$ on Reacher) and against Unpaired contexts on

negative, and one zero. Every conditional 95\% interval includes zero;
the PushT extrapolation interval against Framewise touches it exactly.

over the population of training runs. The completed evidence therefore
does not establish consistent planning superiority or isolate a benefit
from context pairing. Policy-eligible comparisons, with support-only
successes excluded, are reported alongside the raw contrasts.

\paragraph{Why better forecasts need not yield better control.}
In the completed seed-0 development comparison, ShiftWM and Shared context
have equal success rates; Framewise calibration has the highest Reacher
point estimate. A subsequent goal-only intervention keeps the ShiftWM
predictor fixed but substitutes framewise goal correction. Eligible Reacher
success increases from 6/30 to 12/30, compared with 11/30 for the complete
framewise controller; PushT falls from 1/31 to 0/31
(Appendix~\ref{sec:goal-calibration}). This post-hoc intervention motivates
separate checks of prediction and goal alignment, not a general repair.
CEM also selects its own candidate actions, so mean forecast error on
recorded trajectories does not control error on those selected candidates.
Mechanism tests, the completed dynamics-residual revision, and the completed
controlled rollout-objective study are described in
Appendices~\ref{sec:mechanism}, \ref{sec:dynamics-revision}, and
\ref{sec:rollout-revision}.
Matched observed rollouts in Appendix~\ref{sec:qualitative-rollouts}
show successes, failures, and goals reached during support before planning.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
Canonical re-renderings and simulator interventions provide stronger
supervision than uncontrolled real-world video. Frozen coordinates can
omit dynamics-relevant information, and contexts may be ambiguous or
collapse without affecting the reported losses. RGB transformations do not
establish robustness to new camera geometry. The simulated tasks do not
establish arbitrary model/task transfer, real-robot performance, or medical
usefulness. The separate DROID study adds recorded real observations and a
camera-transfer evaluation, but does not measure physical closed-loop control.
On PushT, held-out and extrapolation success point estimates are
below the random-action control; this descriptive comparison does not provide
a matched policy-seed significance test (Table~\ref{tab:planning-controls}).
Matched online-adaptation comparisons remain unfinished. The completed
eight-arm rollout-objective/context study does not satisfy its registered
promotion criterion and is reported separately
(Appendix~\ref{sec:rollout-revision}). Development-driven revisions
are kept separate from the completed primary campaign; their extra
optimization is not a matched-budget result. The original test outcomes have
informed this follow-up work, so any confirmatory revision claim requires
a fresh final holdout and additional training seeds.

ShiftWM makes the observation--dynamics composition hypothesis concrete in
a compact world model. Completed forecasts favor it over framewise
calibration on held-out combinations, while Reacher extrapolation regresses.
The completed planning campaign shows mixed point estimates with conditional
intervals that include zero. Validation-selected local
checkpoints, normalization and history interfaces, and provenance-linked
evaluation records are prepared; public release is pending. The evidence
needed for a stronger claim is improved matched planning together with
controls showing that the separate contexts provide useful information.

\section{Complete experimental protocol}
\label{sec:protocol-details}
\paragraph{Environments and splits.}
The core tasks are visual PushT and Reacher in \texttt{stable-worldmodel}.
They have different control geometries but share infrastructure. Initial
observation factors are canonical, warm, and cool pointwise RGB transforms.
PushT changes space damping (0, 0.15, 0.65); Reacher changes arm and finger
density together (1000, 650, 1350 in simulator units). We train on seven
combinations, reserve $(v_1,p_1)$ for development, and hold out $(v_2,p_2)$
for the main compositional test (Figure~\ref{fig:split}). Test trajectories
are independent across the full grid. Additional dim and physical conditions
test extrapolation separately: dim appearance $v_3$, PushT damping 0.95,
and Reacher density 1500, evaluated at $(v_3,p_0),(v_0,p_3),(v_3,p_3)$.
Each environment has 256 training, 32 validation, 32 development, and 64 test
initial-state seeds. Three physical conditions in the first three splits and
four in test produce 1,216 physical episodes per environment, each with
64 grouped transitions. Appearance versions reuse those physical trajectories.
The full test planning grid has $64\times9=576$ tasks per checkpoint;
the separate extrapolation grid has $64\times3=192$. The all-condition test
average includes familiar factor pairs and is not a wholly unseen-composition
score. This controlled extension is not a reproduction of the original paper's
success table.

\subsection{Planning, eligibility, and resource measurement}
\paragraph{Primary outcomes and uncertainty.}
Closed-loop success under each environment's declared criterion is primary.
We also report steps to goal and worst-condition success under fixed budgets.
Methods use matched initial-state/goal IDs. Core comparisons target three
training seeds; actual seed and episode counts accompany each result.
Episode-level outcomes support paired comparisons; uncertainty over tasks
and variability over training seeds are reported separately. Correlated
frames are not independent trials. Failed runs and timeouts remain in the
record, with a stated policy for their inclusion in denominators.
The main planner uses 300 candidates, 30 CEM iterations, and 30 elites,
matching the released LeWM CEM sampling configuration. This budget is
locked for all model comparisons before full-test learned-policy planning.
It evaluates 9,000 candidate sequences per replan, versus 640 in the
earlier development-only setting; this $14.0625\times$ count ratio is not
a measured wall-time ratio. Both settings use
five grouped actions of prediction horizon, and one group executed per replan.
A group contains five native controls. The 50-step native budget includes
ten steps acquiring initial support. CEM optimizes in the checkpoint's
training action z-score coordinates; it converts candidates to native controls
and clips only physical bounds $[-1,1]$. It does not clip z-scores to that
range. PushT uses the relative-displacement interface from the official
training archive; the earlier absolute-control corpus is quarantined.
Success during support is recorded
separately; policy-eligible success excludes tasks already solved by that
common history. Final-state success is also retained. Per-replan latency
divides actual solve time by actual replanning calls, excluding nonexistent
calls on already-solved tasks. These are configuration facts, not measured costs.
Main efficiency results additionally require dedicated execution with no
concurrent training on the measured GPU; shared-GPU development timing is
retained in raw records but excluded from efficiency tables.

\subsection{Additional diagnostics and task scope}
\paragraph{Diagnostics.}
Completed forecast evaluations measure recursive error in frozen reference
coordinates. Action shuffling, context replacement/removal, and support-length
dependence are additional mechanism tests; their outcomes are not yet reported.
A low one-step error can arise from ignoring actions on predictable
trajectories; action interventions and planning test that failure.
Prediction diagnostics therefore include persistence, zero-action rollouts,
and the change induced by actions. Zero-action predictions are compared
with recorded-action targets only as a sensitivity check, not as errors
against observed counterfactual trajectories. PushT additionally reports a prespecified
subset requiring block translation beyond 20 pixels or rotation beyond
$\pi/9$, so tasks solved by moving the agent alone do not hide manipulation
failures. Both the full set and this subset remain visible.
Within-episode switches are a secondary evaluation after stationary
conditions. Test combinations are never used for hyperparameter selection.

\subsection{Architecture and parameter accounting}
The initial implementation freezes a pretrained LeWM visual encoder and
projector, denoted $E_0$. Context networks, adapters, action encoder,
predictor, and prediction projector are trained. Full visual fine-tuning is
a distinct configuration. The frozen path permits feature caching without
changing inputs or targets. Figure~\ref{fig:method} shows the computation.
The upstream architecture contains 18,034,478 parameters. The ShiftWM
\emph{active inference path} uses 18,319,598, with 12,025,454 trainable
offline. Shared context has 12,025,046 trainable parameters.
The current loaded model object contains 24,873,110 parameters including
the duplicated frozen reference encoder and inactive ablation modules;
the current package serializes that full state. Active-path counts are
not claims about package size or runtime memory, which must be measured
on the actual released artifact.

For $d=192$ and $H=L=3$, both contexts have width 32; the dynamics network
uses a 128-unit GRU. The observation network pools support-feature mean and
population variance. Both adapters use bounded-scale residual FiLM. Inference
holds contexts fixed within a search, so imagined futures cannot modify the
information used to infer dynamics.

Framewise calibration uses the per-image residual
$u+\mathrm{MLP}(\mathrm{LN}(u))$ with dimensions $192\to128\to192$
and an initially zero output layer. It corrects history and goal images
identically, receives no temporal context, and uses the prediction and
canonical-alignment losses. It adds 49,856 active parameters; its active
path has 18,084,334 parameters, of which 11,790,190 are trained offline.
Shared context matches the context-network parameter count approximately;
architecture attribution must still account for computation differences.

\subsection{Exact losses and temporal indexing}
\label{sec:loss-details}
Let $o_t^*=R_{v_0}(x_t)$ be a paired canonical training render and
$z_t^*=\sg(E_0(o_t^*))$ its frozen target. For a sequence indexed $0,\ldots,T-1$, context uses only indices
$0,\ldots,H-1$. Define $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{pred}}=\{H,\ldots,T-2\}$
and $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{align}}=\{H,\ldots,T-1\}$. The implemented query losses are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{pred}}&=\frac{1}{|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{pred}}|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{pred}}}
 \operatorname{mse}(\hat z_{t+1},z_{t+1}^*),\label{eq:predloss}\\
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{align}}&=\frac{1}{|\mathcal T_{\mathrm{align}}|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{align}}}
 \operatorname{mse}(z_t,z_t^*).\label{eq:alignloss}
\end{align}
Here $\operatorname{mse}(u,v)=\norm{u-v}_2^2/\dim(u)$ averages over
feature dimensions, matching the implementation and stated loss weights.
Training uses teacher-forced one-step query prediction. With $T=8$ and $H=3$,
context sees frames 0--2, alignment supervises frames 3--7, and prediction
supervises targets 4--7 from observed history windows. Frame 3 is not a
prediction target in this campaign. Evaluation instead starts immediately
after support and recursively predicts frames 3--7. This boundary and
teacher-forcing mismatch can affect planning even when validation prediction
error is low; the completed runs are not multi-step rollout training.
Frozen targets prevent an encoder from lowering the reported
loss solely by shrinking or changing its coordinates; they do not guarantee
that those coordinates retain every necessary physical quantity.

Appearance variants $S^{(v,p)}$ and $S^{(v',p)}$ of the same trajectory give
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_d=\E\operatorname{mse}(C_d(S^{(v,p)}),C_d(S^{(v',p)})),
 \label{eq:dyncons}
\end{equation}
where $C_d(S)$ abbreviates the corrected-support pathway. Distinct batch
examples with the same observation label give the regularizer
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_o=\E_{v,p,p',S,S'}
 \operatorname{mse}(C_o(S^{(v,p)}),C_o(S'^{(v,p')})).
 \label{eq:obscons}
\end{equation}
The implementation averages over off-diagonal batch pairs with matching
appearance IDs; it does not require their dynamics values or trajectory IDs
to differ. Grouping establishes a shared transformation, not a shared state.
The term does not force state latents of different trajectories to agree. The objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pred}}
 +\lambda_{\mathrm{align}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{align}}
 +\lambda_d\mathcal L_d+\lambda_o\mathcal L_o.
 \label{eq:loss-complete}
\end{equation}
The initial configuration uses $\lambda_{\mathrm{align}}=1$,
$\lambda_d=0.1$, and $\lambda_o=0.01$; unpaired controls disable both context
consistency terms. No additional SIGReg term is used because the target
coordinates are frozen. These choices are configuration facts, not tuned results.
Raw context consistency also has a scale ambiguity: shrinking a context
and increasing downstream sensitivity can lower its penalty without
improving factor separation. Consistency can collapse contexts to constants. Constant-context,
context-shuffle, and removed-pathway controls therefore test whether both
contexts carry useful information. Context swapping across trajectories
is not part of the initial implementation: it requires valid matching targets.

\subsection{Training and checkpoint selection}
The reported campaign uses the fixed encoder and feature-cache path, with
RGB inputs in $[0,1]$, ImageNet channel normalization applied once, and
antialiased bilinear resizing to the released encoder input size. The visual
projector and prediction projector retain pretrained BatchNorm running
statistics. The prediction projector's affine parameters remain trainable;
freezing running statistics prevents query-frame batch statistics from
entering support features and permits batch-size-one planning.
The full campaign trains five variants (Unaligned predictor, Framewise
calibration, Shared context, Unpaired contexts, and ShiftWM), three seeds,
and two environments:
30 runs of 30 epochs,
batch size 128, stride one, and initial learning rate $5\times10^{-5}$.
All 30 configured runs have completed their 30 epochs; the generated ledger
below records the completion evidence. AdamW uses weight decay $10^{-3}$,
gradient clipping at norm 1, and a cosine schedule ending at $10^{-6}$.
Training and validation use BF16 autocast when supported; evaluation uses
FP32. Checkpoints are selected by ordinary-validation prediction MSE, not
last-epoch loss or total loss, whose alignment and consistency terms differ
across controls. Full training completion is distinct from selecting an early
validation-best checkpoint. Main planning evaluation remains separate.

Both training and CEM retain the released-data action normalization. In the
new PushT training corpus, the per-coordinate native-action standard deviations
are approximately 0.504 and 0.504, versus 0.208 and 0.207 in the released archive
(245,760 collected training actions). This distribution difference is retained
in the protocol, not retrospectively removed by renormalizing the completed
campaign. Reacher's corresponding collected and released deviations are both
approximately 0.577. These statistics describe action distributions; they do
not establish that normalization explains the planning failures.

\section{Information access and correctness}
The support must precede query targets. No paired variant of an underlying
trajectory can cross a split. Batch grouping can use factor IDs but model
inputs cannot. Goal images convey target states, not hidden future
trajectories. Future realized actions and rewards cannot enter context
used to select an earlier action.

Checks must establish that saved models reload identically in evaluation
mode; removing adapters recovers the specified base computation; prediction
responds to action changes; support cannot replace query inputs; cached
features match online features under identical transforms; checkpoint
resumes preserve optimizer/scheduler and random state; and paired rendering
does not mutate physical state. These are correctness requirements,
not replacements for training and closed-loop evaluation.

\paragraph{What is not identifiable.}
For $x_{t+1}=x_t+p a_t$, $o_t=v x_t$, and $x_0=0$, observations depend on
the product $vp$. For every $q>0$, $(v,p)$ and $(qv,p/q)$ generate the same
observations under every action sequence. Separate physical interpretation
therefore needs additional assumptions or information. Our contexts are
functional prediction variables, not identified friction or camera
parameters. This elementary example is a limitation, not a theoretical novelty claim.

\subsection{An elementary cost-error decomposition}
This calculation explains why prediction and goal calibration require separate
checks; it is not a theoretical novelty or an empirical guarantee.
For a nonempty finite set of candidate action sequences $\mathcal A$ containing
the returned sequence, let $y(a)$ and $g$ be canonical terminal and goal latents,
and let $\hat y(a),\hat g$ be their model estimates. Define
$\|u\|_d=\|u\|_2/\sqrt d$, so squared normalized norm equals the implemented MSE.
Suppose, uniformly over $\mathcal A$,
\[
 \|\hat y(a)-y(a)\|_d\le e_f,\qquad
 \|\hat g-g\|_d\le e_g,\qquad \|y(a)-g\|_d\le R.
\]
Writing $c(a)=\|y(a)-g\|_d^2$, $\hat c(a)=\|\hat y(a)-\hat g\|_d^2$
and $\varepsilon=e_f+e_g$, expansion of the squared norm gives
\[
 |\hat c(a)-c(a)|\le 2R\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2=:B.
\]
If a returned action sequence satisfies
$\hat c(\hat a)\le\min_{a\in\mathcal A}\hat c(a)+\eta$, then
\[
 c(\hat a)-\min_{a\in\mathcal A}c(a)
 \le 2B+\eta=4R\varepsilon+2\varepsilon^2+\eta.
\]
Indeed, for a true minimizer $a^\star$, apply the cost-error bound twice:
$c(\hat a)\le\hat c(\hat a)+B\le\hat c(a^\star)+\eta+B
\le c(a^\star)+2B+\eta$.
An illustrative scalar counterexample has exact predictions $y(a)=0,y(b)=1$,
true goal $g=0$ and estimated goal $\hat g=1$: goal error alone reverses
the two candidates' ranking. This is a mathematical example, not a measured
simulator result. Empirical mean prediction MSE does not provide the assumed
uniform norm bound; CEM does not certify $\eta$. The statement concerns latent
cost on the stated candidate set, not physical success or causal attribution
for the observed failures.
