\section{Introduction}
A visual world model predicts what will happen after an action. Its last real
observation provides evidence about the scene, but a forecast must also account
for motion and changing conditions. How should a compact predictor use that
evidence, and how much should it reconstruct from its own predictions?

We study these questions through \textbf{ShiftWM}, a family of compact,
action-conditioned world models built on released LeWorldModel components
\citep{maes2026lewm}. The family has two complementary designs.
\emph{Context conditioning} summarizes appearance and observed transitions,
using paired interventions during training to encourage distinct roles.
\emph{Observation anchoring} retains the last measured visual grid and predicts
how to mix and correct its features for each supplied action prefix.
Their representations, supervision and evaluation protocols remain distinct.

The spatial decoder can add a correction to the observation, mix its features
before adding the correction, and optionally bound the correction. Four matched
variants test mixing and bounding. Action-dependent weights change the predicted
state while keeping the measured observation available at every horizon.

Our completed experiments provide three findings. First, on recorded DROID
video, bounded spatial mixing reduces native ten-step endpoint error by
\textbf{5.30\%} versus matched autoregression in a development study. Second,
a complete component comparison attributes the clearest native-coordinate
improvement to the mixing package; bounding and the separate support-context
module have no established incremental native-error benefit. Third, the
context-based variant improves forecasting on held-out appearance--dynamics
combinations in simulation, while its extrapolation and closed-loop planning
results remain mixed.

The contributions are a compact observation-anchored decoder with separately
evaluated components, an intervention-paired context formulation, and a
reproducible evaluation spanning simulated composition and recorded robot video.
Trained checkpoints, source-linked measurements and matched visual diagnostics
support these comparisons. Complete protocols, architectures and outcomes
follow in the attached appendix.

\section{Task and observed evidence}
\label{sec:editorial-task}
Given an observed history and a sequence of supplied actions, predict future
visual features. In the spatial DROID study, the history contains three real
images and two past action blocks; the query contains ten future action blocks.
The image encoder is frozen. Future recorded images provide evaluator targets
and never enter the predictor's support or context. One block contains five
native recorded commands. Figure~\ref{fig:editorial-task} shows this information
boundary using an actual recording.
