\section{Scope, artifacts and next questions}
The results support a specific predictive design: retaining observed visual
features and learning an action-conditioned mixing package can reduce error
under a matched representation and data budget. They do not establish
universal model/task transfer, uniquely identified physical context, physical
robot-control gains or clinical usefulness. The original simulation context
model and real-video spatial models answer related questions through different
protocols. Revised models require a fresh held-out population before a
confirmatory superiority claim; the revealed original and fresh tests remain
excluded from further selection.

All reported runs completed their registered training budgets. Five verified
public releases contain 117 predictors, with source, normalization and
checkpoint manifests. Six additional component predictors are available
locally and pass exact relocated CPU reload checks. Checkpoint availability
is separate from evidence for a particular capability. Feature caches for
new real Box, Rope and PushT studies are prepared and validated; model training
and evaluation on these data remain future work.

ShiftWM's studies separate three claims that otherwise blur together:
observed context can help prediction, retaining the observation can improve
a decoder, and better prediction must still be tested as a basis for action.
The attached appendix preserves the full methods, controls and mixed outcomes
needed to assess each claim.
