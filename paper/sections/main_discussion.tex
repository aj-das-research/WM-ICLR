\section{Scope and reusable artifacts}
The results support a specific predictive design: retain measured visual
features and learn action-conditioned mixing and correction. Fresh held-out
confirmation of the DROID spatial comparison remains needed. The completed IWS
reserved study supports the no-$\tanh$ component across three tasks while
retaining the bounded model's mixed primary outcomes.
The revealed original and fresh tests remain excluded from further model
selection. The completed simulator study finds a substantial Reacher control regression
and no conclusive PushT success improvement. The study does not establish
improved physical control, zero-shot
cross-embodiment generalization or an advantage over independently reproduced
state-of-the-art models.

The spatial release contains all 15 initial predictors, including comparison
arms, with source, normalization and checkpoint manifests. Six additional
component predictors are available locally. All 21 pass exact relocated CPU
reload checks. Earlier releases and other protocols remain in the appendix.
All 36 selected IWS predictors are publicly released with an explicit row-wise
CPU runtime, model cards and verified downloads. Archive-extracted forecasts
match their source packages exactly on independent synthetic inputs; the
feature-input interface and verification scope are documented in
Appendix~\ref{app:experiment-alignment}.

The central finding is that a compact predictor can benefit from continuing
to use the last observed spatial state. Complete ablations, uncertainty and
visual failures identify which parts of this design are supported and which
need further testing.
