\section{Results and interpretation}
\label{sec:editorial-results}
\paragraph{Retaining the observation improves longer forecasts.}
Ours-5 reduces native ten-step endpoint error from 0.203950 for autoregression to
0.193141, a \textbf{5.30\%} relative reduction. It also improves over the
additive anchor (Ours-2) by \textbf{3.55\%}. Both paired intervals exclude
zero. Table~\ref{tab:editorial-spatial} gives the complete native endpoint
comparison; Figure~\ref{fig:editorial-spatial} connects forecast behavior to
the decoder components. On pooled coordinates, gains are smaller: 2.54\%
versus autoregression and an inconclusive 0.47\% versus anchoring.

\paragraph{Mixing drives the clearest component improvement.}
At step ten, adding the mixing package reduces native error by 3.48\% without
bounding and 3.43\% with bounding; both paired intervals exclude zero.
Incremental native bounding effects are inconclusive at both reported
horizons, and every mixing-by-bounding interaction interval contains zero.
Ours-4 has the slightly better five-step point estimate; Ours-5 has the
slightly better ten-step estimate. Two small pooled-coordinate bounding
effects without mixing have favorable intervals. The full factorial table
preserves them alongside all null and unfavorable comparisons.

Ours-5 reduces ten-step endpoint error by 4.50\% relative to the action-free
ablation. Removing the separate support-context path
changes native ten-step error by only 0.10\%, with an interval containing
zero. All four context contrasts are inconclusive. These paths also differ
in active parameter counts: the context-off model has 14.40\% fewer parameters.
Thus the evidence supports the action-conditioned decoder package without
establishing extra benefit from the learned support context.

\paragraph{Context conditioning transfers unevenly across settings.}
The original context model lowers held-out forecast error versus Framewise
calibration by 3.68\% on PushT and 26.78\% on Reacher. Against the closer
unpaired-context control, gains are 2.30\% and 9.21\%. Table~\ref{tab:editorial-context}
keeps the extrapolation outcomes alongside those composition gains.
These are ratios of three-seed means, not significance estimates.
The separate fresh-session real-video confirmation finds a modest 0.742\%
prespecified five-block gain over equally calibrated Framewise, with a paired
interval excluding zero; its ten-block gains are inconclusive.

\paragraph{Forecasting gains do not establish better planning.}
In the original primary planning protocol, held-out raw success is lower than
Framewise by 1.04 points on PushT and 2.08 on Reacher. All twelve paired
raw-success intervals include or touch zero. Planning therefore remains a
mixed result, including after separately reported development revisions.
A goal-only calibration diagnostic shows why predictor accuracy and planning
costs need separate evaluation (Appendix~\ref{sec:goal-calibration}). The
spatial video study supplies no new closed-loop evidence.

\paragraph{What the measured visual examples show.}
Across all 141 validation episodes, 136 have lower native ten-step error
under Ours-5 and five regress. Figure~\ref{fig:editorial-qualitative} displays
both a selected large improvement and a selected failure on their actual
recorded scenes. Each pair compares feature forecasts against the same
withheld target, showing how errors differ on specific recordings;
the complete best/median/worst gallery and population summary remain in
Appendix~\ref{app:spatial-development}. Per-episode records accompany the artifacts.
