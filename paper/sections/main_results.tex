ten-step endpoint error from 0.203950 for matched
autoregression to 0.193141, a \textbf{5.30\%} relative reduction, and improves
over the additive-anchor control by \textbf{3.55\%}. The ten-step contrasts
against autoregression and additive anchoring have paired intervals
excluding zero. Table~\ref{tab:editorial-spatial} gives absolute ten-step errors under four metrics;
Figure~\ref{fig:editorial-spatial} puts temporal behavior and paired gains
side by side. Pooled-coordinate gains are smaller: 2.54\% versus autoregression
and an inconclusive 0.47\% versus the additive anchor.

\paragraph{Ablations identify the clearest contributing package.}
Adding mixing reduces native ten-step error by 3.48\% without bounding and
3.43\% with bounding; both paired intervals exclude zero. Incremental native
bounding effects are inconclusive at both reported horizons, and every
mixing-by-bounding interaction interval contains zero. The unbounded ablation
has a slightly better five-step point estimate; the proposed bounded model
has the lowest ten-step mean among the seven internal learned controls. Its
margin over the closest component, no-$\tanh$ mixing, is only 0.07\%; the paired
interval does not establish a difference. Neither bounded nor
unbounded mixing dominates across horizons. Two small pooled-coordinate bounding effects
without mixing have favorable intervals. The complete component map and all
null or unfavorable contrasts remain in Appendix~\ref{app:spatial-development}.
A new three-seed additive control matches the mixer's active parameter count;
mixing retains its native ten-step advantage (0.193141 versus 0.199812),
while the pooled-coordinate difference remains inconclusive
(Table~\ref{tab:capacity-control}). This post-hoc check supports the decoder
parameterization beyond adding the same number of patch-local parameters.

ShiftWM also reduces ten-step error by 4.50\% relative to its action-free
ablation. Removing the support-context path changes native ten-step error by
only 0.10\%, and all four context contrasts are inconclusive. Context removal
also reduces active parameters by 14.40\%. The evidence therefore supports
the action-conditioned decoder package without establishing added benefit
from the separate learned support context.

\paragraph{What the recorded examples show.}
Across all 141 validation episodes, 136 have lower native ten-step error
under ShiftWM than matched autoregression and five regress.
Figure~\ref{fig:editorial-qualitative} preserves the original largest, median
and smallest episode-gain cases. All methods share the input, withheld target
and error scale. A favorable episode average need not imply a favorable
displayed window. The closest no-$\tanh$ component is shown alongside the
primary method; their small aggregate difference does not support a strong
superiority claim. Per-episode records and the complete replay accompany the
artifacts.

\paragraph{Single-observation transfer and correction range.}
A separate study trains 27 original predictors and nine no-$\tanh$ component
ablations on recorded PushT, Box and Rope. Each model receives one image and
60 native command rows and forecasts 59 feature states. Development results
identify the correction bound as a limitation for this interface
(Table~\ref{tab:iws-unbounded-component}); all 36 checkpoints were fixed before
reserved scoring.

\paragraph{Forecast improvements do not establish better control.}
A separate, completed simulator study trains the current spatial architecture
on PushT and Reacher, with four matched arms, three seeds and 100 held-out
starting episodes per task (2,400 executed trials). All arms share a 50-call
budget including ten support calls and CEM with 128 candidates, eight iterations
and 16 elites. Bounded ShiftWM achieves 11.3\% versus 11.7\% success for
autoregression on PushT (paired difference $-0.3$ percentage points,
95\% interval [$-7.0$, $6.3$]). On Reacher it achieves 27.0\% versus 80.0\%
($-53.0$ points, [$-63.0$, $-42.3$]); the no-$\tanh$ arm achieves 25.3\%.
Thus the recorded-video gains do not establish a generally better planning
model. These separately trained simulator comparisons are not zero-shot robot
transfer. Complete success-by-budget curves, physical errors and measured
online costs appear in Appendix~\ref{app:metric-completion}.
