\subsection{Completing prediction, control and visual evaluation}
\label{app:metric-completion}
The completed simulator experiments test whether the current spatial architecture
improves \emph{decisions}, in addition to forecasting recorded actions. This section
separates completed evidence from additional measurement opportunities. Pending entries are not
results, and the historical context-model simulations do not fill current-model
control cells. New simulator models are trained on simulator training episodes;
this does not constitute zero-shot transfer of a DROID checkpoint.

\paragraph{Prediction and uncertainty.}
Keep absolute native-grid feature errors as the scores; relative reductions
describe a comparison rather than a new metric. Standardized MSE and MAE use
training-only per-coordinate scales. Raw-feature L1 and cosine distance do not
use those scales. Report fixed horizons and paired seed--trajectory uncertainty.
The added DROID metrics are post-hoc secondary analyses on the unchanged
development population, with selected checkpoints frozen. They must reproduce
the finalized MSE records before being accepted. Feature values from different
encoders, token grids or weighting conventions are not directly comparable.

\paragraph{Matching active parameter count.}
A post-hoc control adds a patch-local gated MLP to bounded additive anchoring,
matching the spatial mixer's 1,026,305 active parameters exactly. The added
head never mixes patch positions; the shared upstream encoder is unchanged.
All three new seeds complete the original 30-epoch recipe and selection rule,
with shared original tensors initialized identically to bounded additive.
At native horizon ten, bounded mixing has MSE 0.193141 versus 0.199812 for
this control; their paired difference is $-0.006670$
[$-0.008512$, $-0.004492$]. The pooled-coordinate difference remains
inconclusive ($-0.000291$ [$-0.002315$, $0.002503$]). Intervals use 10,000
paired session--seed bootstrap draws. Thus extra active parameters alone do
not explain the native-grid advantage over this control. This development-only
result does not establish a general expressivity advantage or isolate every
optimization difference. Table~\ref{tab:capacity-control} retains both the
original additive arm and the new matched-capacity arm.

\paragraph{Current-model planning.}
The completed PushT/Reacher campaign compares autoregression, bounded additive anchoring,
ShiftWM and its no-$\tanh$ ablation with matched data and control budgets.
Training, development selection and held-out physical tasks remain separate.
Report native success, terminal physical error, actions to first success with
failures censored at the budget, and complete planning latency. Support actions
count toward the interaction budget. PushT's upstream success combines pusher
and block position with block angle; it is not an overlap score. Reacher's native
criterion is per-joint angular agreement. Supplementary overlap or wrapped-angle
diagnostics retain their own names and units. All 24 models completed training and all 2,400 paired planning trials passed
the complete-study finalizer. Bounded ShiftWM reaches 11.3\% success on PushT
and 27.0\% on Reacher, compared with 11.7\% and 80.0\% for autoregression.
Its paired success differences are $-0.3$ percentage points
[$-7.0$, $6.3$] and $-53.0$ [$-63.0$, $-42.3$], respectively.
Removing the correction bound does not resolve the Reacher failure
(25.3\% success). These results rule out a general control-improvement claim
under this protocol; they do not identify the cause of the failure.

\paragraph{Post-hoc robustness of the control result.}
The Reacher regression occurs for every training seed: bounded mixing trails
AR by 47, 54 and 58 percentage points; unbounded mixing trails by 57, 55 and
52 points. A secondary episode-cluster sign-flip sensitivity analysis averages
within each episode across the three seeds and uses 100,000 random sign draws.
Both Reacher mixing-versus-AR effects retain Holm-adjusted $p<0.001$ across
all eight declared task/contrast tests; no other contrast passes 0.05.
This analysis is conditional on the fitted seeds and assumes symmetric cluster
effects under the null. It does not replace the original paired bootstrap or
constitute a newly held-out confirmation. Figure~\ref{fig:planning-seed-robustness}
shows the original intervals and all seed-specific differences.

\paragraph{Development-only action-ranking experiment.}
To investigate the control failure, we froze a separate diagnostic using eight
metadata-selected development episodes per task, 32 uniform random action
sequences and one zero sequence per episode, and all 24 selected predictors.
Every candidate is executed for 25 native calls from the same restored state
after ten shared support calls; outcomes never change the candidate set.
The experiment evaluates 528 actual candidate rollouts and 6,336 model--candidate
predictions. On PushT, bounded mixing has lower forecast MSE than AR
(0.387 versus 0.428), but a lower mean Spearman correlation between predicted
cost and physical goal distance (0.424 versus 0.635). On Reacher, its MSE is
higher (0.240 versus 0.173) and physical ranking correlation lower
(0.422 versus 0.551). Selected-candidate regret does not uniformly favor AR
(Table~\ref{tab:development-action-ranking}). This separates predictive accuracy
from action ranking but does not fully explain the control gap: only eight
cases are used, random candidates differ from CEM candidates, and the diagnostic
is post-hoc. No test episode, weight, checkpoint selection or primary outcome
is changed.

\paragraph{Shared-decoder RGB endpoint evaluation.}
Following the pixel/perceptual metric families used by IWS and RLA-WM
\citep{wang2026iws,zhang2026rlawm}, we evaluate RGB MSE, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS and
UIQI at stored offset 59 on all 600 reserved handles. Three task-specific readouts
completed 30 epochs using training frames and development-only selection, then
remained fixed across all 36 predictors. Each method uses the same targets,
resize, decoder and metric implementations; raw-image and decoded-feature
persistence are distinct references. Decoding the ground-truth target features
measures readout limitations and is not a forecasting method.
The original IWS 192-step video protocol, masked DINOv3 RLA-WM interface and our
full-image DINOv2 endpoint protocol differ; the numbers are not interchangeable.

The completed 45-row experiment scores 9,000 endpoints. Unbounded mixing improves
LPIPS relative to AR on all three tasks, with all three paired intervals below
zero. Its RGB MSE difference is inconclusive on PushT and Box and unfavorable
on Rope. Bounded mixing has higher RGB MSE than AR on every task. Raw-image
persistence has the lowest LPIPS among the forecasting baselines, even below
the target-feature reconstruction reference. This exposes a readout/metric
limitation: lower feature error does not establish uniformly better visible
predictions. All five metrics and every reference are retained below.

Intervals resample trajectories and predictor seeds jointly, with 10,000 draws,
and remain conditional on the single selected decoder per task. These are
post-hoc, unadjusted diagnostics; no predictor is reselected. An initial exact
feature-MSE replay gate rejected seven of 200 windows for Rope bounded seed 2
(maximum absolute discrepancy $5.96\times10^{-8}$); same-model repeats were
exact. A separately recorded operational revision recomputed every RGB row,
using the existing complementary-metric replay tolerances
(rtol $2\times10^{-5}$, atol $2\times10^{-6}$), and retained all discrepancies.
The initial incomplete results were not pooled into the final study. FID/FVD
and full-clip quality remain unmeasured; endpoint metrics are not proxies for them.

\paragraph{Efficiency and mechanism.}
Table~\ref{tab:iws-predictor-resources} already measures predictor-only latency,
parameters and memory on matched hardware. Encoder-inclusive latency, complete
CEM runtime, training cost and audited FLOPs complete the cost accounting;
unsupported operations must be disclosed by any FLOP counter. Physical-state
probes require a separate training-only fit and held-out labels. Signed error
maps, actual source-mixing weights and correction magnitudes diagnose measured
forecast differences. They are not semantic attention ground truth or evidence
that changing a highlighted pixel would cause a planning improvement.

\paragraph{Task-specific access.}
DINO-WM's Rope/Granular Chamfer distance needs particle states; physical rope
threading needs executed routing trials. Neither can be inferred from IWS Rope
video-feature errors. DROID offline planned-action error needs a separate fixed
inverse-planning protocol and remains distinct from executed robot success.
Access to recorded robot video alone does not provide hardware execution.
