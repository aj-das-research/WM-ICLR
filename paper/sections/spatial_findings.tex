\subsection{What the spatial controls establish}
At the ten-step endpoint, Spatial transport (ours) reduces native feature MSE
by 5.30\% relative to the matched autoregressive predictor
(0.193141 versus 0.203950) and by 3.55\% relative to Anchored additive
(0.200244). Both paired intervals exclude zero. These are relative reductions
in feature error, not percentage-point improvements in task success.
On the original pooled coordinates, the reduction is 2.54\% against
autoregression, while the 0.47\% difference against anchoring has an interval
containing zero. The stronger native-grid result therefore does not establish
an equally strong advantage at every spatial resolution.

Removing the command-conditioning path increases native ten-step error by
the equivalent of a 4.50\% relative reduction for the full model. In contrast,
the separate support-context path adds only a 0.10\% native endpoint point
gain; all four context comparisons have intervals containing zero, including
two small unfavorable pooled-coordinate differences. These controls provide
evidence for the action-conditioned decoder package, but do not establish an
additional benefit from its learned support context. The action-free and
context-off controls also remove active parameters: context-off has 878,497
versus 1,026,305 for the full model (14.40\% fewer). They are information-path
ablations, not exact capacity matches.

Across sixteen registered contrasts, fourteen point estimates favor transport,
nine intervals exclude zero in that direction, and seven intervals contain zero.
Two of the latter have unfavorable point estimates. Because all comparisons
use development validation data and unadjusted intervals, this study supports
further architectural investigation rather than a new confirmatory superiority
claim. The combined decoder changes both feature mixing and innovation bounds;
a separately registered factorial follow-up is needed to distinguish their
individual effects and interaction.
