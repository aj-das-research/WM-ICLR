\subsection{Measured decoder diagnostics}
Figure~\ref{fig:editorial-spatial-method} separates model inputs from withheld
evaluation. In the illustrated DROID window, observed frames have source indices
0, 5 and 10; each action block spans five native source-frame intervals, so
the $h=10$ target is frame 60. These indices denote frames, not seconds.
Figure~\ref{fig:editorial-qualitative} compares the measured forecast errors.
Panel (a) of Figure~\ref{fig:mixing-diagnostics} compares the decoder's internal
weights across four recorded-video settings. Its DROID example is the first eligible
window of the median episode by episode-level gain, retaining the original
selection from Figure~\ref{fig:editorial-qualitative}; its three seeds are
0, 1 and 2. Each IWS example uses training trajectory 000011, initial frame 0,
and command rows 0--59, fixed before inspecting these decoder traces. The
checkpoint snapshot contains PushT seeds 0/1, Box seeds 0/1/2 and Rope seeds
0/2; every included checkpoint completed the full 30-epoch training budget
and passed its individual development-evaluation checks. The other two
mixing seeds were unfinished at this snapshot. This descriptive replay is
separate from the complete-campaign comparison gate and is not evidence of
unseen-data generalization. The initial IWS observation is repeated into the
three temporal input slots, and support-context conditioning is disabled.
The maps preserve each patch's own-source weight, with scalar means across all
16 patches; the archived three-case DROID replay also retains the full source-weight matrices,
observations and predictions. Neither diagnostic establishes physical
correspondence or a causal explanation of forecast error.
Panel (b) gives complementary forecast results for the historical context model
(Appendix~\ref{app:original-context-study}), retaining unfavorable outcomes.
These separate simulator experiments do not evaluate the current spatial
checkpoints. Full gate and retention trajectories remain in the source archive.
