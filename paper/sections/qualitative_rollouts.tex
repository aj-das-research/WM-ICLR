\section{Matched qualitative executions}
\label{sec:qualitative-rollouts}
Figures~\ref{fig:qual-pusht-contrasts}--\ref{fig:qual-reacher-controls}
show actual simulator observations from the completed seed-zero development
evaluations at $(v_1,p_1)$. We select the lexicographically first task in each
outcome stratum relative to Framewise calibration: ShiftWM-only success,
Framewise-only success, and joint failure. The fourth example is joint success
for Reacher; PushT has no eligible joint success, so we explicitly show a
support-only success instead. Selection is by this stated rule, not visual
quality; these outcome-stratified examples do not estimate success frequency.
The accompanying local gallery contains all 64 tasks and adds Shared context.

Both methods receive pixel-identical initial observations and available goals,
with the same planner seed and budget: 300 candidates, 30 CEM iterations,
30 elites, horizon five action blocks, and at most 50 native calls including
support. The first saved frame follows support acquisition; subsequent frames
follow executed blocks of up to five actions. Labels reconstruct exact native
times, including early stopping. Repeated terminal observations do not imply
additional interactions. The world model predicts latents; none of the displayed
images is a decoded prediction, interpolated state, or generated illustration.

\subsection{Contrasting successes, failures, and support-only outcomes}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{generated/qualitative/pusht_contrasts.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{PushT: a success and a contrasting failure.}
Top, episode 2031024: ShiftWM (ours) reaches the scored goal at native call 21;
Framewise exhausts 50 calls. Bottom, episode 2031000: Framewise succeeds at
call 48, while ShiftWM fails despite placing the block within 1.2 simulator
pixels of its target. Its pusher remains 429.0 pixels away, illustrating why
block placement alone does not satisfy this task. The goal is the gray block
and blue pusher configuration in the goal image. The persistent green T is
the upstream renderer's reset marker, not this episode's scored goal.
Position annotations use the native $512\times512$ coordinate system, not
the resized $224\times224$ image. Success requires the combined pusher/block
position-error norm below 20 pixels and block-angle error below $\pi/9$.
These are matched development examples, not evidence of uniform superiority.}
\label{fig:qual-pusht-contrasts}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[p]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{generated/qualitative/pusht_controls.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{PushT: shared failure and success before planning.}
Top, episode 2031001: neither method reaches the scored goal by call 50;
terminal block and pusher errors remain large. Bottom, episode 2031026:
the common support actions already satisfy the goal at call 2. No CEM plan
is executed, and the repeated image is the same saved terminal observation.
This task contributes to raw success but is excluded from eligible policy
success; it provides no evidence of either planner's competence. The gray
block and blue pusher in the goal image define the requested configuration;
the green marker remains unscored renderer context. Pixel errors use native
simulator coordinates. Showing this case makes the distinction between
support success and successful closed-loop control visible.}
\label{fig:qual-pusht-controls}
\end{figure}
