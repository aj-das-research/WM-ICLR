\section{Testing causal support reliability}
\label{app:reliability-development}
We tested whether errors within a longer observed prefix predict when the
factorized forecast should be trusted over Framewise. This is a calibration and
ensembling diagnostic, not a new architectural contribution. The protocol,
code, donor checkpoints and decision rule were frozen before execution;
only original training and validation recordings were used.

\paragraph{Causal prefix backtesting.}
Both frozen donors received their respective original training-fitted residual
calibrations. Within thirteen observed frames, each donor forecast the last ten
from the first three and recorded commands, without incorporating any of those
ten targets into its context or rollout. Let $d$ denote the factorized-minus-
Framewise forecast and $e$ the observed-minus-Framewise residual, both in fixed
training-standardized coordinates. The candidate sets
\begin{equation}
\alpha_s=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}
\frac{\langle e,d\rangle_s+\lambda\alpha_0}
{\langle d,d\rangle_s+\lambda},
\end{equation}
where each inner product averages prefix horizons and feature dimensions.
The zero-denominator fallback is $\alpha_0$.
The prior and shrinkage scale were fitted using original training only;
three-fold training-session cross-validation selected from four fixed
shrinkage values. All three seeds selected $\alpha_0=1$ and $\kappa=100$,
where $\lambda=\kappa$ times training-average donor-disagreement energy.
The base donors had already trained on those sessions, so this procedure
cross-validates only gate fitting, not end-to-end model training.

For the actual query, both donors restarted from the final three observed
prefix frames. Their forecasts were blended with fixed $\alpha_s$, using
recorded future commands and no future images. All gate packages were locked
before decoding validation features. The longer-prefix population contains
758 training episodes in 295 sessions and 132 validation episodes in
57 sessions. Respectively 93 and 11 recordings were too short for the common
23-frame window and remain in the audit. This observation budget differs from
the main three-frame-support experiment. The candidate requires both donors
and two additional prefix rollouts; it is not a single-model improvement.

\paragraph{Failed promotion and complete controls.}
Table~\ref{tab:reliability-development} retains all eleven arms, including
simple averaging, training-fitted constant blends, unshrunk, one-step and
session-shuffled gates. The candidate's h5 reduction versus the global prior
is 0.007\%, with paired MSE difference $-0.00001134$ and 95\% interval
$[-0.00002378,+0.00000256]$. Its h10 reduction is 0.022\%, with interval
$[-0.00008776,+0.00000306]$ for the paired MSE difference.
It fails the prespecified 1\% gain, interval and all-seed conditions.
Ordinary equal averaging has lower mean error than the adaptive candidate;
this does not establish that prefix reliability transfers to the next query.
We therefore do not promote this candidate or test it on the previously
inspected holdouts. Intervals resample sessions and training seeds and remain
exploratory. Table errors use FP64 sufficient-statistic arithmetic on frozen
FP32 donor predictions. The exported FP32 wrapper has exact reload parity;
this is not a claim of bitwise parity with FP64 score arithmetic.
