\section{Confirmatory forecasting on fresh recording sessions}
\label{app:fresh-real-video}
\paragraph{An untouched recording population.}
After the training/validation calibration study, we registered a deterministic
selection of twelve additional official DROID shards, excluding all original
twenty-four shards. The new download contains 556 episodes in 293 site/date
sessions. Before decoding images or running any model, we excluded every episode
whose session appeared anywhere in the original train, validation or test splits:
491 episodes were removed solely for session overlap. The remaining 65 episodes
come from 52 sessions absent from all 433 original sessions, with no original
episode-ID or serialized-record overlap. We did not resample, enlarge this
selection, or filter on outcomes, motion or appearance. Session separation
does not establish distinct scenes or objects.

\paragraph{A frozen comparison.}
Before decoding these observations, we separately froze the evaluation code,
all twelve selected checkpoints, original training normalization, encoder,
twelve train-fitted scalars, metrics and comparisons. We evaluated all four
methods, three training seeds and both original and calibrated variants on
each of two cameras and two horizons: 96 complete evaluations. Every method
receives the same calibration procedure from
Equation~\ref{eq:real-video-residual-calibration}; the scalar is never fitted
on fresh observations. No new neural weights are trained. The original causal
three-frame support, recorded command blocks, feature extraction and stride-five
windowing remain unchanged. Future observations are scoring targets only.
All 65 recordings supply five-block windows. One recording lacks enough frames
for ten blocks; it remains in the manifest and contributes no fabricated query.

\paragraph{Prespecified primary endpoint.}
The sole primary comparison is calibrated ShiftWM (ours) versus equally
calibrated Framewise on camera 1 at the \emph{fifth} predicted frame, measured
in original training-standardized feature MSE. This is an endpoint error,
not the average across five predicted frames. Table~\ref{tab:fresh-real-video-complete}
reports all original and calibrated methods; Table~\ref{tab:fresh-real-video-paired}
preserves their paired gains and uncertainty. Calibrated ShiftWM obtains
0.147654 versus 0.148757 for calibrated Framewise, a
\positivegain{0.742\%} reduction. The paired MSE difference is $-0.001103$,
with 95\% interval $[-0.002145,-0.000290]$. Thus the registered primary
comparison supports a modest improvement on these fresh sessions. It does not
establish a large performance margin or state-of-the-art superiority.

\paragraph{Secondary results and retained regressions.}
Camera-2/five-block transfer yields a 0.522\% point reduction against calibrated
Framewise, with a descriptive interval below zero. Calibrated ten-block gains
are 0.356\% on camera 1 and 0.277\% on camera 2; both intervals include zero.
The original uncalibrated model is worse than Framewise at ten blocks on both
cameras: 0.210635 versus 0.208396, and 0.222992 versus 0.220646,
respectively. These are relative error increases of 1.075\% and 1.063\%,
and are retained rather than replaced by the calibrated outcomes.
Confidence intervals use 10,000 paired session-cluster draws crossed with
training-seed resampling, after averaging windows within each episode and
weighting eligible episodes equally. Only the stated primary comparison is
confirmatory; secondary intervals have no multiplicity adjustment.

\paragraph{Scope of the evidence.}
This separate test supports the reproducibility of a small short-horizon
forecasting gain. Residual contraction is a standard least-squares control,
not a novel architectural or theoretical contribution. Long-horizon superiority
over learned controls remains inconclusive. All input, checkpoint and result
hashes were checked, and endpoint arithmetic was independently recomputed from
every recorded episode. The evidence concerns observational forecasts of real
robot videos; it does not measure closed-loop physical control.

\paragraph{What changes in individual recordings.}
Figure~\ref{fig:fresh-mechanism} connects the calibration rule to recorded
inputs and measured forecast errors. It shows the largest episode improvement,
the median episode and the largest regression, alongside the complete
distribution: 46 of 65 episodes improve and 19 regress. Episode-average gains
need not hold in every window; the fixed first window of the median episode
is one such counterexample. The images are recorded observations, while
prediction quality is measured in the frozen feature space.
