What the numbers say

Three training seeds; equal weight per episode after averaging its windows. Intervals resample sessions and seeds together (10,000 draws), without correction for multiple comparisons. Green marks a positive point estimate; the interval determines whether it is conclusive. The two views share recordings and are not independent datasets.

Why the evidence is still mixed

The primary five-block result improves 2.94% over persistence, but only 0.20% over Framewise, with the latter interval including zero. At ten blocks, error is 1.91% and 1.24% higher than Framewise on the two cameras. These results do not establish a substantial advantage over learned baselines. Validation selected epochs 1–2; later training overfits.

The fixed subset contains 851 training, 143 validation and 132 test episodes. Recording sessions are disjoint; object or scene disjointness is not established. This is a prespecified DROID subset study, not a reproduction of the full DROID policy benchmark.

Two contexts.

Appearance changes and dynamics changes need different evidence.

01

Observation context

Use past visual features to calibrate what the model sees.

02

Dynamics context

Use calibrated history and executed actions to condition future predictions.

Our simulator study uses paired canonical training targets. The real-video variant uses a frozen DINOv2 encoder and recorded-action blocks, without canonical image pairs. Both condition prediction on past support; future target images are withheld from the predictor.

Reusable checkpoints

Twelve real-video predictor checkpoints and a shared frozen encoder have been packaged and checked for offline reload locally. Public checkpoint downloads are not available yet. These checkpoints forecast features; they do not generate RGB videos.
